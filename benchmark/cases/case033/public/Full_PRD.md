# Probabilistic Modeling Product Requirements

The product is a probabilistic programming library for learning from observations and making predictions with uncertainty.

### FP-01: Probability distributions and tensor-valued events

- Given valid distribution parameters, the product draws samples and evaluates log probability mass or density for Bernoulli, binomial, categorical, multinomial, geometric, Poisson, negative binomial, beta, continuous Bernoulli, Cauchy, chi-squared, exponential, Fisher–Snedecor, gamma, inverse gamma, Gumbel, half-Cauchy, half-normal, Kumaraswamy, Laplace, logistic, log-normal, normal, Pareto, Student’s t, uniform, von Mises and Weibull laws. The distribution is the selected mathematical law with the supplied parameters; discrete probabilities and continuous densities are distinguished. Bernoulli and binomial probabilities refer to success, with binomial total count giving the number of trials. Categorical probabilities are normalized category weights, and multinomial events count categories in the supplied total number of trials. Geometric events count failures before the first success. Negative binomial events count successes, with the supplied success probability, before the supplied failure count; positive noninteger failure counts use the usual gamma-function extension of this mass law. Poisson rate is its mean. Beta concentrations are the success and failure shapes, in that order. Continuous Bernoulli has density proportional to the success probability raised to the observed value times the complementary probability raised to one minus that value, normalized over the unit interval; probability one-half gives the uniform law. Gamma uses shape and rate; inverse gamma is the reciprocal of that gamma variable, so its scale equals the supplied rate. Exponential uses rate. Normal scale is standard deviation, and log-normal location and scale are the mean and standard deviation of the logarithm. Cauchy, Gumbel, Laplace and logistic use their location-scale parameterizations; half-Cauchy and half-normal are the absolute values of their centered counterparts. Chi-squared uses degrees of freedom; Fisher–Snedecor uses numerator and denominator degrees of freedom in that order. Student’s t uses degrees of freedom, location and a multiplier of the standard t variable, rather than a standard deviation. Kumaraswamy uses its two positive shape parameters in their conventional order. Pareto scale is its lower bound and its positive shape is the tail exponent. Uniform uses its lower and upper endpoints. Von Mises uses angular location and concentration. Weibull uses scale and shape.

- Multivariate normal, low-rank multivariate normal, multivariate Student’s t, Dirichlet, logistic-normal, Wishart and LKJ distributions represent vector or matrix events with their specified dependence and support. A point-mass distribution always returns its specified event and assigns zero probability to a different event. Multivariate normal location is its mean, with dependence supplied as covariance or its lower Cholesky factor. Low-rank normal covariance is the supplied factor times its transpose plus the supplied diagonal. Multivariate Student’s t uses degrees of freedom, location and a lower Cholesky factor of its scale matrix; this scale matrix is not the covariance. Dirichlet uses one positive concentration per category. Logistic-normal coordinates are independent normal variables with the supplied means and standard deviations. For an event with one more component than normal coordinates, each successive component consumes the fraction of the remaining mass given by the logistic function of its normal coordinate minus the logarithm of the number of event components still to follow; the final component receives the remaining mass. Wishart uses degrees of freedom and a scale covariance matrix. LKJ density on correlation matrices is proportional to their determinant raised to concentration minus one.

- Beta-binomial, Dirichlet-multinomial and gamma-Poisson distributions represent counts after integrating out their beta, Dirichlet and gamma mixing variables, respectively. Zero-inflated Poisson and negative binomial distributions add the caller’s specified extra probability of zero while scaling the original distribution’s remaining mass. The beta mixing variable is the binomial success probability, with success and failure concentrations in that order. The Dirichlet mixing variable supplies multinomial category probabilities. The gamma mixing variable is the Poisson rate and uses shape and rate. Zero inflation is a mixture of a point mass at zero with weight equal to the gate probability and the base count law with the complementary weight.

- A weighted empirical distribution samples among the supplied events in proportion to their weights and, for floating-point events, returns the weighted mean and population variance. Moment requests for integer events are refused as unsupported sample types. A diagonal-normal mixture, including a mixture with shared component covariance, uses the supplied component probabilities, locations and scales. A boolean-selected mixture uses the designated component separately for each batch member. For diagonal-normal mixtures, component log weights give probabilities proportional to their exponentials, component locations are their means and coordinate scales are standard deviations. Shared covariance uses the same coordinate scales for every component. The boolean selector chooses the first component for false and the second for true.

- Distribution sampling preserves separately specified sample, batch and event dimensions. Compatible parameter batches broadcast; expansion changes the batch extent without changing the law at an existing batch member. Reclassifying batch dimensions as one event makes their joint log probability the sum of their individual log probabilities.

- A boolean probability mask makes excluded batch members contribute zero log probability and leaves included members’ scores unchanged. For the missing-value normal and multivariate normal distributions, observations marked as not-a-number contribute no evidence; partially observed vectors are scored using the marginal distribution of their observed coordinates.

- With distribution validation enabled, parameters outside the selected law’s declared parameter domain and ordinary, unmasked scored observations outside its declared support are refused as invalid distribution inputs. Incompatible sample, batch or event dimensions are refused as shape incompatibilities. In particular, normal scale and both beta concentrations are strictly positive, and Poisson rate is nonnegative. Beta observations lie in the closed unit interval, Poisson observations are nonnegative integers, and categorical observations are integer indices from zero through one less than the number of categories.

- For differentiable normal, multivariate normal, beta, gamma and Student’s t sampling, sampled values support differentiation with respect to continuous distribution parameters. Differentiable density evaluation returns derivatives of the selected law at interior points of its support.

- Ordered categorical observations can be sampled and scored from strictly increasing category cutpoints and a real-valued predictor. Each cutpoint specifies a baseline cumulative log-odds, from which the predictor is subtracted; there is one more category than cutpoint.

- Projected-normal directional samples have the distribution of the direction of an isotropic unit-covariance normal vector with the supplied location. Directional log densities are available in dimensions two, three and four; scoring in other dimensions is refused as unsupported.

### FP-02: Learnable parameter state

- A named learnable parameter is initialized on its first registration and subsequently returns its current value. Supplying a different initializer under an existing name does not replace that value; a deferred initializer is evaluated only for a new parameter.

- Parameters can be constrained to real values, positive values, bounded intervals, probability simplexes, positive-definite matrices and lower-triangular matrices with positive diagonals. Reads return values in the declared constrained domain, and differentiable learning can update the parameter without leaving that domain.

- Callers can inspect, replace and delete named parameters and clear all parameter state. Reading an absent parameter without an initializer is refused as a missing parameter.

- Saving and restoring parameter state preserves the learned values and their constraints. Restored parameters remain usable for further learning.

- An isolated parameter scope has its own named values and constraints. Leaving that scope restores the surrounding parameter state, and entering a previously retained scope resumes its own state.

### FP-03: Executable probabilistic models

- A probabilistic program can combine named random choices, observations, deterministic calculations, data-dependent branches and loops. Unobserved choices are drawn from their supplied distributions; an observed choice returns the supplied observation and contributes its likelihood to inference.

- A partially observed random choice preserves events marked observed and samples the remaining events from the supplied distribution. A mask that cannot broadcast over the distribution’s batch dimensions is refused as an incompatible observation mask.

- A caller-supplied log probability factor contributes that amount to the model’s log probability. Recording a deterministic quantity exposes its value without changing the model’s probability.

- Resetting the random seed reproduces subsequent stochastic results for the same program, inputs, device and execution environment. Seeding covers the program’s standard-language, numerical-array and tensor random draws. A locally seeded execution restores the surrounding random state on exit.

- Callers can enable or disable validation globally or for a temporary scope. On leaving a temporary scope, the previous validation choice applies again.

### FP-04: Execution inspection and probabilistic program control

- Recording a probabilistic execution exposes its named random choices, observations, parameters, deterministic quantities and return value. Probability evaluation reports individual site contributions and the joint log probability. Reusing a random-choice name within one recorded execution is refused as an ambiguous site.

- Conditioning fixes selected random choices to supplied values and makes them observed evidence. Removing observation status makes those sites random choices again. Replaying a previous execution reuses selected recorded latent values without making them observations; unselected sites retain their model-defined behavior. An observation supplied by the model takes precedence over a replayed latent value at that site. Conditioning with a supplied nonmissing value replaces a model-supplied observation at the selected site.

- An intervention supplies a selected value to downstream calculations without treating that intervention as evidence about its parents. The execution record retains the factual draw or observation and its probability contribution while downstream calculations use the intervention value.

- Callers can hide selected sites from surrounding execution inspection while preserving their contribution to the wrapped program’s returned value. A parameter substitution supplies chosen parameter values within the wrapped execution without overwriting the stored parameter values.

- A positive probability scale multiplies selected log probability contributions, and a boolean evidence mask excludes designated contributions. Nested scales multiply and nested masks retain only contributions allowed by every enclosing mask. With validation enabled, nonpositive scales are refused as invalid probability weights.

- Placing a caller-specified prior over a selected learnable parameter turns its use into a random choice governed by that prior, which can be observed, replayed and recorded as a latent quantity.

### FP-05: Conditional independence and minibatch semantics

- A caller can declare repeated conditionally independent variables, with nested groups representing independent axes. Vectorized groups broadcast compatible distributions to the declared group size; sequential groups permit data-dependent control flow within individual members.

- Selecting a minibatch of an independent population returns its selected members and scales enclosed log probability contributions by the population size divided by the selected batch size. Caller-supplied selections are respected.

- Data and learnable parameters can be subsampled consistently with the selected independent-group members. Declared event dimensions remain intact; a parameter without a batch-dimension declaration is not subsampled.

- Incompatible group and distribution sizes, conflicting axes for simultaneously active independent groups, and disagreement between a declared minibatch size and its explicit selection are refused as inconsistent independence declarations.

- A caller can declare a finite history of dependence in a repeated Markov computation. Inference preserves dependence on that history and treats separate independent sequences as separate populations.

- Batched categorical indexing selects the entries designated by each index while broadcasting independent index batches. The selected values remain correct when indices carry additional enumeration or independent-group dimensions.

- For an executed model path, dependency inspection identifies random-choice values used to determine later probability distributions. By default it omits deterministic quantities, point masses and observations suppressed by a scalar exclusion mask. Prior dependencies include each site itself and its directly used latent parents. Posterior dependencies include each latent site itself, its direct children and later latent parents of those children, with later defined by the executed model order. The result distinguishes dependence confined to corresponding members of an independent group from dependence across members; a shared observation depending on an aggregate of group members couples those members in the posterior.

### FP-06: Bayesian neural modeling

- A neural model can expose ordinary learnable weights and constrained learnable attributes to probabilistic learning while retaining its ordinary forward computation. Nested neural components retain distinct parameter identities, and repeated access to the same named parameter uses its current learned value.

- A neural model can replace selected attributes with prior-distributed random quantities, including priors dependent on other attributes. Repeated reads of a random attribute during one model invocation return the same sampled value; a new invocation can draw a fresh value. Observations, replay and interventions can control these random attributes.

- Neural parameter state can be saved and restored so that the restored component produces the same deterministic predictions from the same input. Probabilistic components retain the distributions specified by their restored learned parameters.

- Trainable dense and autoregressive neural mappings produce caller-sized outputs, including mappings conditioned on external context. In an autoregressive mapping, an output for one input coordinate depends only on the coordinates earlier in the specified ordering and on the context; changing a later coordinate does not change that output.

- A Bayesian hidden layer accepts input activations, independent normal weight uncertainties and a chosen activation function. For each input row, its output has the marginal distribution induced by those weight uncertainties and the activation function. The default activation replaces negative activations with zero. By default an additional constant-one activation is appended; the caller can disable that addition. Its probability score contributes the negative caller-weighted divergence between the weight distribution and a zero-mean normal weight prior. The default weight prior has unit variance.

### FP-07: Transformed and conditional probability distributions

- A caller can form a distribution by applying an ordered sequence of transformations to a base distribution. Samples follow the resulting transformed law, and density scores account for the change in volume on the transformation’s domain. Elementwise affine transformations multiply by the supplied scale and add the supplied location; exponential and hyperbolic-tangent transformations apply their respective mathematical functions.

- Learnable flow families include affine autoregressive, affine coupling, batch normalization in evaluation mode, block autoregressive, neural autoregressive, planar, polynomial, radial, spline, spline autoregressive, spline coupling and Sylvester flows. Their generated samples and associated log densities depend differentiably on their learned transformation parameters. An autoregressive transformation has an ordering in which each output depends only on its own input and preceding inputs. Affine autoregressive transformations are positive affine functions of their own coordinate, with location and scale depending on preceding inputs. Affine coupling leaves the first selected block unchanged and applies positive affine functions to the remaining coordinates, with coefficients depending on the unchanged block. By default the first block contains the integer part of half the input coordinates. Evaluation-mode batch normalization is a positive, coordinatewise affine transformation. Block autoregressive transformations are compositions of block-triangular affine maps with positive diagonal blocks and increasing nonlinear activations; each output is increasing in its own coordinate. Neural autoregressive transformations apply an inverse activation to a positive, normalized mixture of activated positive affine functions of their own coordinate; the mixture coefficients, slopes and offsets depend only on preceding coordinates. Supported neural activations are the logistic sigmoid and hyperbolic tangent. Polynomial autoregressive transformations have coordinate derivatives that are sums of squares of polynomials in that coordinate; their coefficients depend only on preceding coordinates. Callers can supply that coefficient function and select the polynomial degree and number of squared terms; the transformation is the integral from zero to the coordinate of that squared-polynomial sum, plus a learnable offset. Planar transformations add a vector multiplied by the hyperbolic tangent of an affine scalar projection, with projection of that vector greater than minus one. Radial transformations send an input to itself plus beta times its displacement from a learned center divided by alpha plus distance to that center, with alpha positive and beta greater than minus alpha. Sylvester transformations add an orthogonally rotated, triangular linear combination of hyperbolic tangents of a triangular affine map in rotated coordinates; the paired triangular diagonal products exceed minus one. Spline autoregressive transformations use coordinatewise monotone splines whose coefficients depend on preceding coordinates; spline coupling uses coordinatewise splines on one block and context-dependent splines on the remaining block, conditioned on the first block.

- Affine autoregressive, affine coupling, spline, spline autoregressive and spline coupling transformations support forward and inverse evaluation on their domains. Forward and inverse evaluation recover the original value, and reported local volume changes agree with the transformation’s derivative.

- Conditional flows accept an external context that determines the transformation and hence the conditional sample law and density. Conditional affine autoregressive, affine coupling, neural autoregressive, planar, radial, spline and spline autoregressive families support this use; conditioning preserves the caller’s context batch distinctions. Conditioning selects a member of the corresponding unconditional mathematical family for each context.

- Support transformations map real-valued coordinates into positive values, ordered vectors, probability simplexes, positive-definite matrices, correlation matrices and triangular matrix domains, with inverse mappings on their interiors. Composition preserves the caller’s transformation order.

- Spline flows support linear rational and quadratic rational splines within a caller-specified positive bound, and leave values outside that bound unchanged. A nonpositive bound or a spline order other than these two is refused as an invalid transformation configuration. Within the bound, each coordinate is a continuous increasing piecewise rational function mapping the two boundary endpoints to themselves. A quadratic rational segment is a ratio of quadratic polynomials interpolating its two endpoint values and positive endpoint derivatives. A linear rational spline splits each bin at an interior point and uses ratios of linear polynomials on the two parts, with matching values and first derivatives. Adjacent bins share their value and derivative at a knot. Bin count controls the number of these interpolation bins; widths, heights, derivatives and linear split positions are learnable. Coordinatewise splines have no cross-coordinate dependence.

- Reparameterized models used for inference reconstruct values in the original variable domains and represent the original model’s probability law in the new coordinates. Supported uses include location-scale decentering, transformed continuous variables, Student’s t variables, stable variables, projected-normal variables and invertible time-coordinate changes. For location-scale decentering with centering fraction c, auxiliary location is c times the original location and auxiliary scale is original scale to power c; reconstruction adds to the original location the original scale to power one minus c times the auxiliary value minus its auxiliary location. Transformed variables apply the supplied transformation sequence to base coordinates. Student’s t uses gamma precision of shape and rate half the degrees of freedom and a conditional normal with the original location and original scale divided by square root precision. At stable exponent two, independent uniform angle on minus pi over two to pi over two and unit-rate exponential radius noise reconstruct location plus twice scale times sine of the angle times square root radius noise. Projected-normal coordinates normalize concentration plus independent standard-normal noise to unit length. Time-coordinate changes apply the orthonormal type-II discrete cosine transform or orthonormal Haar transform along the selected axis of unconstrained support coordinates; Haar optionally reverses that axis before transformation. Reconstruction uses the inverse time transformation and then the support mapping. Scores in augmented coordinates are joint auxiliary densities, rather than the original marginal density, with the appropriate support-coordinate volume change.

- A caller can split an event dimension into parts for separate inference treatment. For supplied part values whose lengths cover that dimension, the reconstructed event and its probability score agree with the original unsplit event. Splitting an observed event with lengths that do not cover the chosen dimension is refused as an incompatible partition.

### FP-08: Optimization and resumable training state

- A caller can optimize named learnable parameters using a supplied optimizer and its settings. Parameters first encountered after training has begun participate in subsequent updates; parameters not selected for an update retain their values.

- Optimizer settings can differ by parameter. A zero learning rate leaves the selected parameter unchanged, and gradient clipping acts separately on each selected parameter tensor. Value clipping truncates gradient coordinates to the supplied symmetric absolute bound; norm clipping scales a gradient exceeding the supplied Euclidean-norm bound to that bound, preserving its direction up to numerical precision.

- Learning-rate schedules change subsequent update rates according to the supplied step schedule or monitored metric. Newly encountered parameters use the schedule associated with their optimizer.

- Saving and restoring optimizer state, together with the corresponding parameter state, permits training to continue with the same update history. State supplied before a parameter is encountered applies when that named parameter is subsequently optimized.

- Disjoint parameter groups can use different optimizers within one objective. Assigning one parameter to more than one group is refused as an overlapping optimization assignment.

- For a twice-differentiable scalar objective over one-, two- or three-dimensional parameter vectors, a caller can request a second-order update and optionally constrain its distance from the current value by a positive trust radius. For a strictly convex quadratic objective without an active distance restriction and with nonsingular, numerically resolved curvature, the update reaches its minimum and returns the inverse curvature as its covariance estimate. The update remains differentiable with respect to objective inputs when the objective has the additional derivatives needed for that differentiation.

### FP-09: Variational learning and automatic posterior approximations

- Given a model, a guide describing its latent variables and observations, variational learning evaluates the negative evidence lower bound and provides gradients for model and guide parameters. The objective is the guide expectation of its log probability minus the model’s joint log probability; both continuous and discrete latent choices contribute to the objective and its gradient.

- A learning step applies the selected optimizer to the participating model and guide parameters and returns the objective value. Objective evaluation without an update leaves learned values unchanged. The objective estimate averages the caller-selected number of independent stochastic draws.

- For a model with fixed continuous latent structure, automatic guides provide point estimates, independent normal approximations, full-covariance normal approximations, low-rank-plus-diagonal normal approximations and transformed normal approximations. Generated latent values respect each original site’s support. Independent, full-covariance and low-rank normal guides expose their normal-coordinate medians and requested marginal quantiles mapped to the original domains; for real and positive scalar sites these are the corresponding marginal summaries in the original domains.

- A caller can combine automatic and custom guides over disjoint latent sites. Structured guides can express dependencies among sites using point-mass, univariate normal or multivariate normal conditionals, or caller-supplied conditionals; automatic Gaussian guides retain dependencies permitted by the model’s conditional-independence structure. For the structured conditional families, caller-selected initialization values locate the conditionals in unconstrained coordinates. The supplied initial scale is the initial standard deviation for the normal coordinates; the multivariate conditional initially has independent coordinates at that scale. A caller-supplied conditional supplies its own perturbation law. Dependency functions add their results on upstream unconstrained perturbations to downstream perturbations, before adding the downstream location and mapping to the original support.

- Guide initialization can use caller-provided per-site values or a caller-provided initialization rule. Continuous automatic guides refuse discrete latent sites as unsupported latent domains; a discrete automatic guide learns separate probabilities for Bernoulli, categorical and one-hot categorical sites configured for vectorized enumeration. Other discrete laws and sequential independent groups are refused by that discrete guide as unsupported guide configurations.

- With inference validation enabled, model and guide disagreement about a shared site’s event dimensions or event shape is refused as an incompatible latent specification. A guide probability factor lacking the caller’s declaration of whether its source is differentiably sampled is refused as an incomplete guide specification.

- A point-estimate guide optimizes posterior density in the original constrained latent domain.

- Likelihood-free learning accepts a static generative model with differentiable observation samples and a guide whose latent samples are all differentiable, even when observation density evaluation is unavailable, provided the predictive distribution has finite absolute moments of the selected exponent. The predictive energy score is the expected powered Euclidean distance from a generated observation to the supplied observations, less half the expected powered distance between independent generated observations. Distances include all observed coordinates; evidence masks exclude coordinates and probability scales weight their squared distances. The finite-draw score averages data distances and distances between distinct draws. Optional prior regularization subtracts the caller-weighted sum of model latent log probabilities over those draws. The exponent defaults to one, the regularization weight to zero and the draw count to two. Exponents outside the open interval from zero to two, negative regularization weights, draw counts below two and models without observations are refused as invalid objective settings. At zero regularization weight, learning does not require model or guide density evaluation.

- A learned point estimate in unconstrained latent coordinates can be converted into a local Gaussian posterior approximation when the negative joint log density has positive-definite curvature there. The approximation is centered on that estimate and has covariance equal to the inverse of that local curvature.

### FP-10: Finite discrete marginalization and posterior states

- For finite discrete latent variables declared enumerable, inference can integrate their probability mass instead of treating one selected value as the entire latent uncertainty. For nested independent groups, with an enumerated choice used only in its own group context or a nested context and with no model-enumerated choice outside a group enclosing a guide-enumerated choice, the resulting marginal evidence and evidence lower bound account for all those states with their probabilities.

- Guide enumeration supports both sequential choices with data-dependent branching and vectorized choices whose subsequent calculations support batched values. Model-side marginalization supports vectorized enumeration; requesting sequential model-side marginalization is refused as an unsupported enumeration mode.

- Given observations and enumerable latent states, discrete posterior inference either draws joint latent assignments from the conditional posterior or returns a joint assignment of maximum posterior probability. The default is posterior sampling; other temperatures than zero for maximization and one for sampling are refused as unsupported inference choices.

- Discrete marginal inference can return the posterior marginal probabilities of individual enumerated model sites.

- Enumerated dependencies can include finite-history Markov sequences. Inference accounts for transition and observation evidence across the sequence when producing marginal evidence or posterior assignments.

- Vectorized enumeration requires dimensions separate from active independent-group dimensions. A nonnegative starting dimension is refused as an invalid enumeration dimension. Model-side marginalization requires scalar probability scales shared by the marginalized sites; unequal scales or scales with batch dimensions are refused as inconsistent probability weighting.

### FP-11: Posterior sampling and sequential filtering

- A caller can request posterior draws for continuous latent variables conditioned on model observations, with configurable retained draw counts, warmup and multiple chains. Returned values are in the latent sites’ original constrained domains, and warmup draws are excluded from retained posterior samples. Chains can be retrieved separately or combined; individual draws are combined across chains by default. Caller callbacks receive each warmup and retained draw in order, distinguishing the stages and chain and numbering draws from zero within each stage.

- Continuous posterior sampling accepts either a probabilistic model or a caller-supplied differentiable negative log density with initial values. Model-based sampling supports constrained continuous variables and marginalization of enumerable discrete variables. A gradient-free sampling option supports model-based continuous inference whose initialization has a finite density and available finite derivatives; subsequent sampling transitions do not require density derivatives.

- The caller can supply initial latent values and retain only selected latent sites. A caller-supplied negative log density without initial values is refused as missing initialization. Distinct caller-supplied initial states for multiple chains are supported through the sequential-chain sampling option. Such initial values must provide one state per chain; an incompatible chain count is refused as inconsistent initialization. Failure to find a finite valid initial density and the finite derivatives needed by initialization is reported as an initialization failure.

- Importance sampling draws proposals from a supplied guide, or from the model’s prior when no guide is supplied, and associates each draw with its model-to-proposal probability ratio. Normalized weights determine empirical posterior probabilities, and the average unnormalized weight estimates model evidence.

- Sequential filtering accepts an initial state and successive observations through a model and proposal guide. Each observation updates a weighted distribution over the retained state; correlations among components of an individual state remain represented. A guide may read but may not overwrite the model’s retained state.

- Sequential filtering refuses a nonpositive particle count and reports failure when every candidate state has zero probability under the new evidence.

- Posterior sampling can return aggregate counts, means and unbiased sample variances without returning retained individual draws. Variance divides the sum of squared deviations from the sample mean by one less than the retained count. Caller-selected streaming accumulators can additionally provide covariance summaries. These aggregates describe the requested post-warmup sampled quantities and are separated by chain by default.

### FP-12: Predictive distributions and posterior diagnostics

- Given a model and either posterior latent samples or a guide, prediction draws the remaining random quantities conditional on those latent values. Without posterior information, prediction produces prior predictive samples. Returned deterministic quantities and model returns correspond to the same generated executions.

- Callers can select which sites to return. By default prediction returns sites absent from supplied posterior samples; when a guide supplies posterior samples, its latent sites are also returned. If a nonempty supplied posterior sample set conflicts with a requested draw count, its common sample count takes precedence and the conflict is reported as a warning.

- Prediction refuses simultaneous use of a guide and a nonempty posterior sample set as conflicting posterior sources. If neither a draw count nor posterior samples provide a sample count, prediction is refused as an incomplete request.

- Weighted prediction returns generated predictive values and their model-to-guide probability ratios for the supplied observation-conditioned model. When separate prediction and evidence models are supplied, generated quantities follow the prediction model while probability ratios use the evidence model.

- Given posterior samples, callers can obtain means, standard deviations, requested quantiles, central probability intervals and highest-density intervals at a requested probability mass. Each summary describes the selected sample dimensions while preserving the remaining event dimensions. Unweighted quantiles interpolate linearly between adjacent ordered observations with the minimum at probability zero and maximum at probability one. Central intervals leave equal probability in both tails. For requested mass strictly between zero and one, a highest-density interval is a shortest interval between ordered sample values whose endpoint ranks differ by the integer part of the sample count times the requested mass. Reported standard deviations are sample standard deviations: centered squared deviations are summed over the selected samples and divided by their count minus one before taking the square root.

- For samples separated into chains, convergence diagnostics expose effective sample size and between-chain versus within-chain variation, including split-chain diagnostics. Autocorrelation summaries describe dependence at requested lags, so correlated samples and separated chain centers remain visible in the diagnostics. For a nonconstant coordinate, empirical autocovariance at each lag is the sum of products of sample-mean-centered pairs separated by that lag divided by the number of overlapping pairs. Empirical autocorrelation divides each of these autocovariances by the zero-lag autocovariance.

- Given predictive samples and observations, continuous ranked probability score is the mean absolute distance from a predictive sample to the observation, less half the mean absolute distance between two independently drawn samples from that same equally weighted empirical distribution. For at least two samples with positive weights and requested probabilities strictly between zero and one, weighted quantiles interpolate between ordered sample values. Each value’s probability position is its cumulative normalized weight, shifted and rescaled so the first and last positions are zero and one.

### FP-13: Sequence distributions and temporal state models

- A discrete hidden Markov model accepts initial state probabilities, transition probabilities and state-dependent observation distributions, and returns sequence log probabilities with hidden states marginalized out. Its first observation follows the first transition from the initial state. Filtering returns the final hidden-state distribution conditioned on the entire observation sequence.

- A linear Gaussian hidden Markov model accepts initial uncertainty, linear transitions, linear observations and Gaussian process and observation noise. It produces distributed observation sequences, marginal sequence log densities and the final hidden-state posterior mean and covariance. Conditioning on an observed prefix provides the conditional distribution of the remaining observations. The probability model uses row-vector states: the next state is the preceding state right-multiplied by the transition matrix plus process noise, and its observation is that next state right-multiplied by the observation matrix plus observation noise. Initial uncertainty and all process and observation innovations are mutually independent.

- Sequence distributions accept constant or time-varying transition and observation parameters with compatible batches. A fully time-homogeneous sequence sampler needs a supplied duration; absent duration is refused as an unspecified sequence length.

- Gamma-scaled Gaussian hidden Markov models represent a shared uncertain precision: all Gaussian initial, process and observation covariance matrices are divided by the same gamma-distributed precision. They return the integrated sequence likelihood, the posterior distribution of the precision and the final-state posterior mean. Gaussian Markov random fields return normalized observation-sequence likelihoods from their specified Gaussian initial, transition and observation factors.

- Linear state models with differentiably sampled non-Gaussian initial, transition and observation noise generate sequences according to those supplied noise laws. Independent groups of sequence distributions can be combined into multivariate observations without introducing dependence between the groups. All three non-Gaussian noise laws must support differentiable sampling; an input noise law without that capability is refused.

- For regularly spaced multivariate time-series data, built-in trainable models provide independent Matern processes, linearly mixed Matern processes, dependent Matern processes, linear Gaussian state-space models and linear Gaussian state-space models with Matern observation noise. They return data log likelihoods and conditional forecast means. For both linear state-space model families, forecasting is supported with zero observation location; learnable nonzero observation location is supported for likelihood evaluation and learning. Forecast uncertainty is covariance among outputs at each horizon for independent and linearly mixed processes and ordinary linear Gaussian state-space models. For dependent Matern processes, the uncertainty promise covers output marginal variances, including the full variance in a single-output model. For a linear state-space model with Matern observation noise, forecast covariance is supported when the linear state contributes nothing to the observations. Forecasts include fresh observation noise and condition on all supplied observations. For Matern process models, forecast times are offsets from the last observation; state-space forecasts use subsequent integer steps.

- Independent Matern processes are stationary, zero-mean Gaussian processes, one per output. Kernel scale is marginal standard deviation, length scale is measured in the same units as time, and observation noise is independent centered normal noise with the supplied standard deviation. Let scaled distance be the square root of twice the smoothness order times absolute elapsed time divided by length scale. The Matern covariance is squared kernel scale times the exponential of negative scaled distance, multiplied by one for order one-half, one plus scaled distance for order three-halves, or one plus scaled distance plus one-third its square for order five-halves. Linearly mixed models use independent processes of this law, with each row of the supplied mixing matrix giving that process’s coefficients in the outputs.

- Dependent Matern processes are stationary second-order Gaussian processes. For each coordinate, the second time derivative plus twice its decay rate times the first derivative plus squared decay rate times the value equals correlated white-noise forcing. The decay rate is the square root of three divided by that coordinate’s length scale. The forcing covariance per unit time is the supplied lower noise factor times its transpose. Independent normal observation noise has the supplied standard deviations.

- Linear Gaussian state-space models have zero initial mean and diagonal initial covariance equal to the supplied initial variances. State transitions and observations use the row-vector matrix convention, with independent centered Gaussian process noise having the supplied diagonal variances. Observation noise has the supplied standard deviations and a location that is zero unless learnable observation location is requested. The model with Matern observation noise additionally has independent stationary Matern processes, one per output, added to the observations independently of the linear state and observation noise. Its continuous time unit equals one discrete state transition.

- Likelihoods are differentiable with respect to continuous trainable parameters.

- Independent and linearly mixed Matern time-series models support smoothness orders one-half, three-halves and five-halves; the dependent Matern model supports three-halves. Other orders are refused as unsupported temporal covariance choices.

- On small, well-conditioned double-precision sequence and temporal models, numerical log probabilities, posterior means, supported covariances and continuous likelihood derivatives agree with the mathematical model within absolute error two times ten to the minus seven plus relative error two times ten to the minus six. This applies to sequences of at most thirty-two steps and at most eight hidden and eight observed coordinates, with finite log probabilities and derivatives, parameter magnitudes at most ten, and nonsingular Gaussian covariance condition numbers at most ten thousand.

### FP-14: Gaussian process learning and prediction

- Given training inputs, real-valued observations, a covariance function, an optional mean function and Gaussian observation noise, Gaussian process regression returns the conditional mean and uncertainty at new inputs. The caller can request marginal variances or full predictive covariance and can include or exclude observation noise. The default mean is zero and the default observation-noise variance is one; predictions default to marginal variances excluding observation noise.

- Covariance choices include squared exponential, exponential, rational quadratic, Matern three-halves, Matern five-halves, cosine, periodic, linear, polynomial, Brownian, constant, white noise and coregionalization. Covariances can be summed, multiplied, exponentiated, scaled by input-dependent factors or evaluated on warped inputs; the resulting covariance respects the supplied composition. Let r be Euclidean distance after dividing each selected coordinate difference by its positive length scale, and v the covariance variance. Squared exponential covariance is v times exp of minus r squared over two; exponential is v times exp of minus r; rational quadratic is v times the power minus a of one plus r squared over twice a, where a is the positive scale-mixture concentration. Matern three-halves is v times one plus square root three times r, times exp of minus square root three times r. Matern five-halves is v times one plus square root five times r plus five r squared over three, times exp of minus square root five times r. Cosine is v times cos of r. Periodic covariance is v times exp of minus twice the sum of squared sine of pi times each coordinate difference divided by its period, each sine divided by its length scale before squaring. Linear covariance is v times the selected-coordinate inner product. Polynomial covariance is v times the supplied positive bias plus that inner product, raised to the supplied positive integer degree. Constant covariance equals v.

- Sparse regression accepts inducing inputs and supports deterministic training conditional, fully independent training conditional and variational free-energy approximations. The variational free-energy approximation is the default; unrecognized approximation choices are refused as unsupported regression configurations. In the deterministic conditional approximation, training function values have no residual uncertainty after conditioning on inducing values. The independent conditional approximation retains each training point’s conditional residual variance but no residual covariance between training points. The variational free-energy objective uses the deterministic conditional likelihood with a penalty equal to half the total discarded training variance divided by observation-noise variance. New-input function values retain their full conditional uncertainty given inducing values, with residuals independent of training residuals. Predictions use the selected approximation and inducing locations; when inducing inputs cover the training inputs, predictions recover the corresponding full regression result up to numerical precision.

- Variational Gaussian process models support Gaussian, binary, multiclass and Poisson observations. Gaussian observations add independent normal noise to latent function values. By default, binary observation probabilities are the logistic response of latent values, multiclass probabilities are normalized exponentials across classes, and Poisson rates are exponentials of latent values; callers may supply a response mapping into the required probability or rate domain. Full and inducing-input posterior approximations are multivariate normal laws over training or inducing function values, with the supplied location and covariance equal to the supplied lower scale factor times its transpose. Predictive function moments integrate the Gaussian-process conditional law given those values against the fitted approximation. Their locations and uncertainty are trainable.

- A Gaussian process latent-variable model treats training input coordinates as independent standard-normal latent values and learns independent normal posterior locations and scales from observed outputs under the selected Gaussian process observation model. The caller chooses the latent dimension and initial coordinate locations; learned locations and uncertainty remain available for dimensionality reduction.

- Training data can be replaced while retaining learned covariance and likelihood parameters. Inconsistent numbers of training inputs and observed outputs, or prediction inputs with incompatible feature dimensions, are refused as incompatible Gaussian process data.

- Covariance functions operate on the caller-selected input coordinates, using all declared coordinates by default. Scalar variances, length scales and periods default to one; polynomial degree and bias and rational-quadratic scale-mixture concentration also default to one. Brownian covariance uses one coordinate: it is the variance multiplied by the smaller absolute coordinate on the same side of zero and is zero on opposite sides. White-noise covariance is diagonal for a single input collection and zero between explicitly separate collections, even at equal coordinates. Coregionalization uses the bilinear covariance defined by the supplied factor covariance plus the supplied nonnegative diagonal covariance.

### FP-15: Probabilistic forecasting and backtesting

- A forecasting model combines a prediction determined by covariates and latent variables with a residual probability distribution. Given an observed history and covariates covering that history and a future horizon, it returns joint samples for the unobserved future, preserving dependence across forecast times and observation dimensions.

- Forecast fitting supports a variational posterior or a sampled posterior over model latent variables. Variational fitting accepts a custom guide and reports its training losses as negative evidence lower bound per observed scalar data element.

- Hierarchical forecasting supports conditionally independent groups of series and minibatches of those groups. Each series retains its own observations and covariates while sharing the model’s declared global uncertain quantities.

- Training observations and training covariates must cover the same duration. At prediction, covariates must cover at least the observed duration, and the requested forecast sample count must be a positive integer. Violations are refused as incompatible forecasting inputs.

- Backtesting uses each eligible split between a training interval and its immediately following evaluation interval. By default training expands from the beginning of the data, evaluation extends to its end, both minimum interval lengths are one time point, and splits advance by one time point. A fixed training or evaluation length overrides the corresponding minimum; the first split has the required training length and the last leaves the required evaluation length. A supplied positive stride determines the separation between splits. No eligible split returns no evaluations. Results identify both intervals and the requested metrics. Reusing fitted variational state with a moving fixed-length training window is refused as an incompatible warm-start request.

- Built-in forecast metrics are mean absolute error of the predictive sample median, root mean squared error of the predictive sample mean, and continuous ranked probability score. Mean absolute error averages absolute errors over evaluated scalar observations; root mean squared error takes the square root after averaging squared errors over those observations. Continuous ranked probability score uses the equally weighted empirical predictive distribution and is averaged over evaluated scalar observations. All three are returned by default, using one hundred forecast draws per split unless the caller selects a different count. Caller-supplied metrics receive predictions and the corresponding held-out truth.

### FP-16: Stochastic epidemic modeling

- A caller can define a discrete-time stochastic compartment model by specifying compartments, initial counts, transition distributions and observation distributions. Prior simulation generates compartment and observation trajectories and can fix supplied parentless global quantities.

- Built-in epidemic families include susceptible–infected–recovered, susceptible–exposed–infected–recovered and a version that also accounts for deaths; overdispersed and superspreading variants of the first two; time-varying susceptible–infected–recovered; sparsely observed cumulative infections; unknown infection start; regional susceptible–infected–recovered; and its time-varying regional variant. Their trajectories follow the selected compartment transitions and conserve the modeled population, counting recovered people implicitly when they are not returned.

- Given observed infection data, fitting produces approximate posterior samples for unknown global epidemiological quantities and latent histories. Prediction after fitting reconstructs unobserved compartments and generates the requested number of future time steps, retaining the number of fitted posterior draws. Prediction without fitted samples is refused. For an identifiable one-step binomial infection probability with a beta prior and known binomial reporting probability, posterior fitting must resolve both the posterior mean and its uncertainty: the mean has absolute error below 0.12 and sample variance is between one tenth and five times the independently integrated posterior variance, with sufficient fitting effort and at least 160 posterior draws.

- Sparse cumulative-infection observations constrain the accumulated reported infections at observed times while unobserved times are inferred. Each step adds a binomially reported subset of new infections to the cumulative count. Observation masks distinguish missing times from observed zero counts. This model supports inference retaining discrete compartment counts; a continuous relaxation of its exact cumulative observations is outside the supported fitting capability.

- A compartment model requires distinct compartment names and a positive integral observation duration. A scalar population must be an integer of at least two; regional population entries must each be at least one. Invalid duration, population or duplicate compartment names are refused as invalid epidemic model declarations.

- The basic epidemic models start with one infected person and all others susceptible, with no exposed or dead people. In each time step, a susceptible person is infected if at least one current infected person transmits; the per-pair transmission chance is the reproduction number divided by recovery duration and population. Newly infected people enter the exposed compartment when that compartment exists and otherwise enter the infected compartment. Existing exposed and infected people progress independently with probabilities equal to the reciprocals of their respective incubation and recovery durations. The count of reported new infections is binomial with the new susceptible departures as its total and the reporting probability as its success chance. The death variant first assigns deaths among current infected people with chance mortality fraction divided by recovery duration, then recovers a binomial subset of the surviving current infected people. These probability laws describe interior transmission probabilities up to numerical precision.

- Except where a variant specifies otherwise, the reproduction number has a log-normal prior whose logarithm has mean zero and standard deviation one, and the reporting probability has a beta prior with both concentrations ten. Recovery and incubation durations are greater than one time step; a death-model mortality fraction is strictly between zero and one. Values outside those ranges are refused as invalid epidemiological inputs.

- The overdispersed variants replace each binomial transition and reporting count with a beta-binomial count of the same mean. Their shared dispersion has a beta prior with concentrations two and six. For an interior success probability, the mixing probability has variance equal to the square of dispersion times the square of success probability times the square of failure probability. Zero dispersion gives the binomial law. The superspreading variants instead use beta-binomial infections with first concentration equal to the superspreading concentration times the current infected count and second concentration chosen to preserve the combined transmission probability. The superspreading concentration has an exponential prior of rate one. For those variants the per-pair transmission chance is the reproduction number divided by population, and both variants use the current infected count as their infectious count. Other transitions and reporting remain binomial.

- The time-varying single-population model multiplies the baseline reproduction number by a multiplier initially one whose logarithm receives independent zero-mean normal increments of standard deviation one tenth each step. Its reporting probability is constant over each of three successive periods of fourteen, seven and sixty steps, with independent beta priors of concentrations two and four, four and four, and eight and four respectively. Simulation and forecasting for this variant are supported within that total eighty-one-step period.

- The unknown-start variant starts with everyone susceptible and adds a caller-selected positive number of steps before the recorded observations. Those preceding steps have zero reported infections. External exposure adds an effective infectious count equal to recovery duration divided by the product of reproduction number and preceding-window duration, allowing infections before any internal infected person exists. Separate reporting probabilities before and during the observed period have independent beta priors with both concentrations ten; forecasting retains the latter probability. Predictions include the first infection time measured from the beginning of the added preceding period.

- Regional models accept per-region populations and a coupling matrix and start with one infection in the first region. For each destination region, the infectious count and population used in its infection probability are the sums of source-region counts weighted by the corresponding source-to-destination couplings; its susceptible count and recovery law remain local. Coupling weights lie between zero and one. The destination infection law is supported where its coupled population is positive. The constant regional model uses region-specific beta reporting probabilities with two shared concentration parameters having independent gamma priors of shape ten and rate one. The time-varying regional model instead uses independent beta baseline reporting probabilities with both concentrations four, a shared multiplicative transmission process initially one, and separate regional reporting log-odds shifts initially zero. Transmission log increments and reporting shifts are zero-mean normal increments with separate shared standard deviations, each with a log-normal prior of log mean minus three and log standard deviation one.

### FP-17: Bayesian experiment design

- Given a probabilistic model accepting a candidate design, selected future observation sites and selected latent targets, experiment-design evaluation estimates the expected information gained about those targets by observing the selected observations. The quantity is the expected reduction from prior target entropy to conditional posterior target entropy under that design. Sample budgets count independent outer joint draws and inner integration draws per observation. For a balanced binary target observed through a binary symmetric channel whose crossover probability lies between ε and 1−ε, where 0 < ε < 1/2, the nested estimate has the following concentration guarantee. Averaging K independent estimates, each using N outer and M inner draws, has absolute error at most t−log(1−2(1−2ε)u), except with probability at most 2 exp(−2KNt²/log²((1−ε)/ε)) + 2KN exp(−2Mu²), for positive t and u with 2(1−2ε)u < 1. Independence gives zero information exactly.

- Batched candidate designs receive separate expected-information estimates, preserving the design batch dimensions. Nuisance latent variables influence the observation distribution but are not treated as information targets unless the caller selects them.

- Caller-supplied posterior, marginal-observation and conditional-observation approximations can be trained for information estimation. Posterior-based evaluation can return average posterior entropy instead of information gain, and training-based estimators can return their requested objective histories. The nested variational estimator requires differentiably sampled latent approximation distributions and no unselected nuisance latent variables. Estimation using only a marginal-observation approximation likewise requires no unselected nuisance latent variables; the paired marginal-observation and conditional-observation approximations can account for them. Other information estimators retain their supported discrete-target capability. Posterior evaluation is cross-entropy: the expectation under model-generated target–observation pairs of negative log probability of the target under the supplied conditional approximation. Information is prior target entropy minus that cross-entropy; an inexact approximation need not give the true information. Posterior histories are supported for the average-entropy result, while information results for this estimator are supported without histories. Marginal-observation evaluation is expected conditional observation log likelihood minus log marginal approximation density. Paired-observation evaluation replaces the conditional likelihood by the caller’s conditional approximation density. Each is an empirical mean over the requested joint sample population. Training without histories minimizes the relevant negative approximate log likelihoods; paired training minimizes both approximation cross-entropies. For nested variational evaluation the observation marginal is the empirical mean of joint model density divided by conditional approximation density on independent conditional approximation draws; evaluation is the outer empirical mean of conditional observation log likelihood minus log of that marginal. Nested training minimizes the negative log empirical marginal. Objective histories use the parameter state before each update and report evaluation objectives on that step’s sample population. For paired-observation estimation, histories report the evaluation objective but do not promise simultaneous improvement of the conditional approximation; learning that approximation is supported without histories.

- Information evaluation also accepts a trainable score for observation–target pairs. It can return a density-ratio information estimate or a variational information lower-bound estimate. The lower-bound interpretation applies to the expected objective, not to every finite random estimate. Density-ratio evaluation is the empirical joint-population mean of the supplied score, averaged over sampled target populations when separate target and observation budgets are supplied. Without histories, its logistic training objective is mean softplus of negative joint score plus softplus of independent-pair score. With histories it reports the mean-score evaluation objective separately for each sampled target population, without a learning-improvement guarantee. The variational lower-bound objective is mean joint score minus logarithm of the empirical independent-pair mean exponential score. Its training maximizes that same objective. Independent pairs use the product of the target and observation marginals; these objectives evaluate the supplied score even when it is an imperfect density ratio.

- After training an information approximation on one batch of candidate designs, callers can evaluate it on a separately supplied batch of final designs with a separately supplied evaluation sample count. The returned information estimates describe the final designs. Final posterior-approximation evaluation uses that fitted approximation’s cross-entropy on model draws at the final designs, or prior target entropy minus that cross-entropy when information is selected. The separately requested evaluation budget determines the evaluation population; it is not the training population reused.

- Training an information approximation is refused as a nonfinite-objective failure if its aggregate training objective becomes not-a-number.

### FP-18: Probabilistic tracking and detection association

- Given a target state mean, covariance and numerical time, tracking predicts a Gaussian state at a requested later time under nearly constant position or nearly constant velocity dynamics. Nearly constant position preserves the mean and adds independent position variance equal to the supplied velocity variance times the square of elapsed time. Nearly constant velocity advances each position by its velocity times elapsed time and preserves mean velocity. The predicted covariance is the covariance of that linear motion plus independent per-axis motion noise. In the discrete-noise variant, added position variance is acceleration variance times the fourth power of elapsed time divided by four; in the continuous-noise variant the divisor is three. Both velocity variants add velocity variance equal to acceleration variance times squared elapsed time and position–velocity covariance equal to acceleration variance times cubed elapsed time divided by two.

- A position measurement at the predicted state time returns the conditional Gaussian state under independent, zero-mean Gaussian measurement noise of the supplied covariance. The innovation is measured position minus predicted position; its covariance is the predicted position covariance plus measurement covariance, and its log likelihood is that Gaussian observation density. A measurement at a different state time is refused as a time mismatch.

- Time-based tracking uses states with a numerical time. Omitting both a time and a frame designation is refused as missing time information. A prediction requires exactly one of an elapsed time and a destination time; supplying both or neither is refused as an invalid time request.

- Given candidate-object log existence factors and detection-to-object log assignment factors, association inference returns posterior existence probabilities and detection assignment probabilities, including spurious detections. A feasible joint hypothesis assigns each detection either to one existing object or to no object; the unframed model permits multiple detections for one object. Its relative probability is the product of the exponentiated factors for all existing objects and all assigned detection–object pairs. Absent objects and spurious detections contribute unit factors. Normalizing these hypothesis probabilities defines the joint association law.

- For persistent objects observed in multiple frames, association probabilities account for shared existence across frames and permit at most one detection per object within a frame.

- Sparse unframed association accepts an explicit set of feasible detection-to-object edges. Excluded edges receive zero assignment probability, while each detection retains the possibility of being spurious.

- Exact association inference is the default for dense unframed and persistent models and returns marginals of their joint association law. Approximate inference returns normalized assignment probabilities and object-existence probabilities for the same factors. For an unframed feasible-edge graph without cycles and finite feasible log factors of magnitude at most ten, approximation effort at least the total number of object and detection vertices returns marginals within absolute error one millionth of the exact joint-law marginals.

### FP-19: Causal treatment-effect estimation

- A latent-confounder causal model learns from real-valued covariates, binary treatment assignments and observed outcomes. The confounder has independent standard-normal coordinates. Given it, covariates have independent normal uncertainties and treatment is Bernoulli; the outcome follows the selected family conditional on confounder and treatment, with separately learnable response parameters for treated and untreated individuals. The fitted approximation has a Bernoulli treatment distribution conditional on covariates, the selected outcome family conditional on covariates and treatment, and independent normal confounder coordinates conditional on covariates, treatment and outcome. Fitting minimizes negative variational evidence together with negative approximate conditional log likelihoods for the observed treatment and outcome, and returns training losses per individual.

- Outcome laws are Bernoulli, exponential, Laplace, normal and Student’s t; Bernoulli is the default. An unrecognized outcome family or nonpositive feature, latent or hidden dimension is refused as an invalid causal model configuration.

- Given covariates after fitting, individual treatment-effect estimation estimates, under the fitted approximate posterior for the confounder given covariates, the expected outcome under treatment minus the expected outcome without treatment for each individual. The requested posterior sample count controls that conditional average, rather than necessarily averaging all treatment and outcome uncertainty. For a finite-variance intervention contrast, its estimate is unbiased for the fitted approximate-posterior expectation and has variance no greater than the variance of its conditional mean across treatment and outcome plus its mean conditional variance divided by the requested sample count.

- Treatment-effect prediction accepts new individuals with the training feature dimension and processes caller-selected batches without changing which individuals the returned effects describe. A covariate matrix with a different feature dimension is refused as incompatible causal prediction data.

- Saving and restoring a fitted causal model preserves its learned treatment-effect distribution for the same covariates.

### FP-20: Biological sequence learning

- Biological sequences can be read from an in-memory sequence collection or FASTA records using the built-in amino-acid alphabet, the built-in DNA alphabet or a caller-supplied alphabet. The amino-acid alphabet contains alanine, arginine, asparagine, aspartic acid, cysteine, glutamine, glutamic acid, glycine, histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, proline, serine, threonine, tryptophan, tyrosine and valine; the DNA alphabet contains adenine, cytosine, guanine and thymine. Symbols absent from the selected alphabet represent missing observations but still count toward sequence length. The default alphabet is amino acids, and stop-symbol addition is disabled by default. The resulting dataset preserves sequence membership and individual lengths while providing a common padded length; by default that length is the longest sequence after optional stop-symbol addition.

- Missing categorical observations in a sequence contribute no emission evidence. The missing-data hidden Markov distribution evaluates variable-length sequence probabilities and returns at each position either the hidden-state probabilities conditioned on the observation prefix through that position or the hidden-state probabilities conditioned on the whole sequence, as requested. Unlike the general discrete sequence distribution, its first observation is emitted directly from the initial state, before any transition.

- Biological sequence models represent substitutions, insertions and deletions relative to a latent sequence. A profile model learns a shared sequence profile; a factor model learns per-sequence latent coordinates with either a normal or Laplace prior. Fitting returns variational learning losses and permits evaluation on separate sequence datasets.

- Sequence evaluation returns a sum of local variational evidence estimates across the evaluated sequences. Each local estimate scores the observed sequence under the fitted model; for a factor model it also includes the latent-coordinate prior score minus the corresponding guide score.

- Sequence perplexity is the exponential of the average, over sequences, of negative local variational evidence divided by that sequence’s recorded length.

- A fitted factor model returns a posterior location and positive scale for each sequence’s latent coordinates, with the caller-selected latent dimensionality. Its conditional coordinate approximation has independent normal or Laplace coordinates matching the chosen prior family. Padded data length, explicitly supplied latent sequence length, observed alphabet size and latent coordinate dimensionality must be positive integers; values that are not positive integers are refused as invalid sequence model dimensions.

- Generated categorical sequences can be written as FASTA records using the supplied alphabet. Padding is excluded when writing without stop truncation. The caller can append records or replace the destination contents and can request truncation at the first stop symbol. With stop truncation, padding exclusion is supported for sequences containing a stop before padding; sequences without a stop are supported when they contain no padding.

- The sequence profile has ordered precursor positions and insertion positions before, between and after them. At each next precursor position, an insertion decision can emit from the insertion distribution without consuming the precursor; otherwise a deletion decision either skips that precursor or emits from its residue distribution. Insertion and deletion probabilities depend on the position and whether the preceding action was a match, insertion or deletion. The initial preceding action is a match before the first position. After the last precursor, emissions use the final insertion distribution. Sequence likelihood sums over all alignments consistent with this model; no separate sequence-length probability is included.

- The shared profile places independent normal priors on categorical log weights, normalizing their exponentials to probabilities. Residue log weights have mean zero. Each insertion and deletion decision has a no-event log weight with prior mean ten by default and an event log weight with mean zero; the common prior standard deviation defaults to one. The caller can change that standard deviation and the no-event prior mean. Its learned approximation has independent normal uncertainty for these log weights.

- Factor-model latent coordinates have independent zero-mean unit-scale normal priors by default or unit-scale Laplace priors when selected. Residue log weights are affine functions of these coordinates followed by a common positive sharpness multiplier and normalization of exponentials. Affine weights and offsets have independent zero-mean normal priors, each with standard deviation one by default. Sharpness is the logarithm of one plus the exponential of a normal quantity with standard deviation one and mean one hundred by default. With relevance determination enabled, each coordinate has an independent gamma scale of shape and rate one that divides the prior standard deviation of its affine weights. Relevance determination is disabled by default.

- Factor-model insertion and deletion probabilities are shared across sequences by default, with the profile prior for indel decisions. Optionally their log weights are affine functions of latent coordinates with the same no-event prior bias. Substitution is enabled by default: positive substitution weights have independent zero-mean normal logarithms of standard deviation ten by default, map latent residue probabilities to observed-alphabet weights, and the resulting observed weights are normalized. Disabling substitution identifies the latent and observed alphabets. The default latent alphabet has the observed alphabet size, and default latent sequence length is the integer part of one point one times the padded data length. Callers may change those sizes and prior scales.
