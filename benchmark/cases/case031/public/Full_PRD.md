# Power and Energy System Analysis Requirements

The product is a framework for analysing power and energy systems, optimising their operation and investment, and comparing their technical and economic outcomes.

### FP-01: Network construction and component lifecycle

- Users can create and inspect networks containing buses, carriers, generators, loads, lines, transformers, links, processes, storage units, stores, shunt impedances, global constraints, geographical shapes, and line and transformer types. Component names identify individual assets within their component category; carrier names can represent user-defined energy sectors.

- Users can add individual assets or groups of assets with shared or asset-specific static attributes. Duplicate names within an addition are refused. Existing assets are retained unless replacement is explicitly requested; replacement removes the replaced assets' old time series as well as their old static attributes. A refused addition, removal, or snapshot, investment-period or scenario assignment leaves the network unchanged.

- Removing an asset removes its static and time-varying data, across all scenarios. Removing a name that is absent is refused. Renaming buses updates references to those buses in other components, including bus scopes of global constraints, without altering equal names used in other roles.

- An independent network copy preserves component data and network metadata, and subsequent edits to the copy do not change the source. A selection by buses retains one-port assets on those buses and two-terminal branches whose connected buses are retained.

- Asset data from networks with disjoint asset names can be merged, preserving attributes shared with the receiving network. The receiving network's metadata and reusable electrical type definitions are retained. Overlapping names among transferred assets are refused; a merge that includes time series requires identical snapshots and snapshot weights.

- Electrical active power and power capacity are measured in megawatts, reactive power in megavars, passive-branch capacity in megavolt-amperes, energy in megawatt-hours, nominal bus voltage in line-to-line kilovolts and elapsed time in hours. Bus voltage angles are in radians; transformer phase shifts and their bounds are in degrees. Line resistance and reactance are in ohms and shunt admittance in siemens; transformer impedance and admittance are relative to its own rated apparent power and its terminal nominal voltages. Costs use a consistent caller-chosen currency.

- A newly created network has one snapshot and no user assets. Unspecified load active and reactive demand are zero. Generator dispatch for optimisation is unrestricted by a fixed target unless one is supplied; generator reactive-power targets default to zero and conversion efficiency defaults to one. Nominal power and energy capacities initially equal zero; assets are active, not extendable, not committable and not maintainable unless requested otherwise. Bus nominal voltage initially equals one kilovolt and its carrier is alternating-current electricity. Users can retain additional descriptive attributes on assets.

### FP-02: Time, scenario and characteristic-curve data

- Users can assign nonempty ordered snapshots and attach asset-specific time series aligned to those snapshots. A supplied time series takes precedence over the static value for that asset and attribute. Input series and calculated result series remain separately accessible. Inputs whose dimensions or labels do not match the requested assets and snapshots are refused.

- Snapshot weights separately represent contributions to operating cost, generator energy accounting, and storage elapsed hours. Each weight defaults to one. For timestamp snapshots with at least two entries, users can derive elapsed-hour weights from successive timestamp differences, with the last snapshot using the preceding interval's duration.

- Empty snapshot selections and snapshots supplied as a timezone-aware timestamp index are refused. Hierarchical snapshots contain exactly an investment-period level and a timestep level; other numbers of levels are refused.

- Investment periods are unique, strictly increasing integers. Assigning periods to a network with a single time axis repeats its existing time series and snapshot weights for each period. Periods assigned to an existing period-and-timestep axis must match its periods; incompatible period assignments are refused. Separate period weights represent objective contributions and years represented, both initially one. An empty period assignment leaves the network unchanged.

- Users can introduce named uncertainty scenarios and scenario-specific component data. Existing component data is initially replicated into each scenario. Supplying only scenario names assigns equal probabilities; explicitly supplied probabilities must sum to one within an absolute tolerance of 0.00001, or the assignment is refused. Replacing an already established scenario set is unsupported and refused.

- Users can extract a scenario as an independent deterministic network while retaining its component data and investment-period weights. Users can select snapshots or investment periods while retaining the selected component time series and snapshot weights.

- Users can attach and inspect piecewise component characteristics as quantity breakpoints with associated cost, efficiency or conversion-rate values. The represented curve is independent of breakpoint input order, and assets may have different numbers of breakpoints. Combining uncertainty scenarios with piecewise component data is refused as unsupported.

- Changing snapshots preserves values at retained snapshots, removes discarded snapshots and supplies attribute defaults at newly introduced snapshots for existing input time series. Existing snapshot weights are retained unless elapsed-hour derivation is requested; newly introduced weights use the supplied common weight, defaulting to one.

### FP-03: Network consistency and electrical topology

- Consistency checking identifies assets that reference absent buses or carriers, disconnected buses, zero line resistance or zero line reactance, capacity lower bounds above upper bounds, energy lower bounds above upper bounds, and phase-shift lower bounds above upper bounds. The default is to warn except that phase-shift-bound violations are refused by default. Users can select strict checks; an unknown strict-check selection is refused.

- Topology analysis groups buses into connected subnetworks using passive lines and transformers. Controllable links do not join these subnetworks. Users can inspect bus adjacency and the orientation of branch connections, with parallel branches remaining distinguishable.

- Users can define reusable line types from resistance, reactance and capacitance per kilometre and nominal frequency, and transformer types from rated power, short-circuit voltage, resistive short-circuit voltage, no-load loss, no-load current and tap characteristics. Line-type resistance and reactance are in ohms per kilometre, capacitance in nanofarads per kilometre and frequency in hertz. Transformer-type rated power is in megavolt-amperes, no-load loss in kilowatts, and short-circuit voltage, resistive short-circuit voltage, no-load current and tap step are percentages. Applying a type overrides the corresponding individually supplied electrical characteristics. Line resistance and reactance scale with length and inversely with the number of parallel circuits; capacitance scales with both length and parallel circuits and determines susceptance at the supplied frequency. A transformer tap changes the voltage ratio by the specified percentage step for each position away from neutral. An absent referenced type is refused. Reapplying types after an edit updates the derived electrical characteristics.

- Scenario consistency checking refuses differences in connected buses, component type, carrier, injection sign, aggregation weight, extendability, module size, fixed capacity target, commitment eligibility, build year, lifetime, active status, transport delay shorter than the represented horizon or cyclic delivery between scenarios for the same asset. Line and transformer type data must also agree across scenarios. Type differences warn by default and refuse when the corresponding type check is selected as strict.

- Users can assign summer and winter line ratings to timestamp snapshots. Summer is April through September unless different months are selected. The seasonal rating relative to nominal capacity multiplies an existing availability limit by default; users can instead replace that limit. Non-datetime snapshots, absent line names, missing seasonal ratings, nonpositive ratings or nominal capacities, and an empty summer-month selection are refused.

- For a transformer type, the resistive short-circuit percentage gives series resistance relative to rated power, and the total short-circuit percentage gives the magnitude of series impedance. No-load loss gives shunt conductance relative to rated power; the no-load current percentage gives total shunt-admittance magnitude, with inductive susceptance. If the supplied no-load current magnitude is smaller than the conductance magnitude, susceptance is zero. Parallel transformer units divide series impedance and multiply shunt admittance, while the type supplies the rated power, phase shift and tapped side.

### FP-04: Network persistence and external data import

- Users can export and reload deterministic networks through CSV, netCDF, HDF5 and Excel, retaining component identities, static attributes, time series, snapshots, weights, investment periods, piecewise component data and supported network metadata. Excel metadata round-tripping covers scalar values and nested dictionaries. Reloaded data preserves the represented network, including attributes omitted from storage because they equal their defaults.

- netCDF persistence preserves uncertainty scenarios and their probabilities, geographical shapes and the default geographical coordinate reference system. Exporting a stochastic network to CSV, HDF5 or Excel is refused as unsupported.

- Users can read and write network data in local storage. An operation requiring unavailable optional format support fails with a missing-support indication.

- Users can import version-two electrical cases expressed as bus, generator and passive-branch tables with a supplied base power, retaining voltage bases, injections, controls, impedance, branch ratings and transformer ratios in the network's units. Users can also import external tabular distribution networks containing buses, loads, generators, shunts, lines and two-winding transformers. Electrical fidelity for this distribution-network import covers lines and transformers specified by reusable electrical types.

- Excel import reads the network's component and time-series worksheets and ignores unrelated worksheets.

- In version-two electrical-case imports, a passive branch is a transformer when its terminal nominal voltages differ, it has a tap ratio other than zero or one, or its phase shift is nonzero; other branches become lines. For positive base power and nominal voltages, line impedance equals supplied per-unit impedance times squared nominal voltage divided by case base power, and line susceptance equals supplied susceptance times case base power divided by squared nominal voltage. Transformer series impedance is rebased by rated apparent power divided by case base power, and transformer shunt admittance by the inverse ratio. A zero transformer tap ratio denotes unity. Branch ratings are copied; zero ratings remain zero unless the caller supplies a replacement. Generator nominal capacity comes from maximum active output; prescribed active and reactive output are retained. Generator control follows its source bus control, and a generator voltage target takes precedence over its bus voltage target. Nonzero bus demand creates a load; nonzero bus shunt power creates a shunt whose admittance is that power divided by squared nominal voltage. Source component service status does not deactivate imported components.

- In tabular distribution-network imports, bus nominal voltage and component connections are retained. Load active and reactive demand and prescribed generator powers include their supplied scaling factors. Voltage-controlled generators retain active output and voltage targets; fixed-injection generators retain active and reactive output; external-grid connections become slack generators. External-grid voltage targets take precedence over generator voltage targets at the same bus. Shunt active and reactive power are represented by conductance and susceptance at the supplied shunt nominal voltage, with positive reactive consumption represented by negative susceptance. Typed lines retain length and parallel-circuit count; typed transformers retain their connected high- and low-voltage buses and reusable type selection.

### FP-05: Linear and nonlinear static power flow

- For specified active-power injections, linear power flow returns passive-branch flows and bus voltage angles in AC subnetworks, with unit voltage magnitudes. For a line whose terminals have the same nominal voltage, flow from the first bus to the second equals squared nominal voltage times their angle difference divided by line reactance. For a transformer, this flow equals rated apparent power multiplied by the difference between the terminal angle difference and phase shift, with both angles expressed in radians, divided by the product of per-unit reactance and tap ratio. In DC subnetworks, voltage-magnitude deviations replace angles and resistance replaces reactance. Each subnetwork's slack injection balances its net mismatch; the slack angle or voltage-magnitude deviation is zero. Linear electrical quantities are accurate within absolute 0.000001 megawatts or megavars.

- Nonlinear AC power flow returns bus voltage magnitudes and angles, active and reactive injections, and active and reactive power at both ends of passive branches. Terminal power is positive when withdrawn from the corresponding bus and negative when supplied to it. Results satisfy AC electrical balance with resistance, reactance, shunt admittance, transformer taps and phase shifts, accounting for electrical losses. Electrical balance residuals are accurate within absolute 0.000001 megawatts or megavars. Lines have half their shunt admittance at each end. Transformers support a central shunt with half the series impedance on each side, the default, or shunts split equally between their ends with the series impedance between them. A transformer tap can apply at either terminal, defaulting to the first; tap ratio defaults to one and phase shift to zero. A standalone shunt with positive conductance consumes active power and one with positive susceptance supplies reactive power, with both powers proportional to squared voltage magnitude.

- In nonlinear AC analysis, a bus with prescribed active and reactive injection retains those injections; a voltage-controlled bus retains prescribed active injection and voltage magnitude; the slack bus retains its voltage magnitude and angle and supplies the residual active and reactive power.

- Users can distribute active-power slack across generators according to dispatch, nominal capacity, optimised capacity, or supplied bus and generator participation weights. When participation is based on supplied bus weights, co-located generators divide a bus's participation share in proportion to nominal capacity. Optimised-capacity participation follows each bus's optimised capacity and divides that share among co-located generators in proportion to their nominal capacities. Capacity-based participation with no positive participating capacity is refused. Electrical balance and normalised participation results are accurate within absolute 0.000001 in their stated units.

- Nonlinear AC analysis distinguishes convergence from nonconvergence for each requested snapshot and subnetwork and reports the remaining power mismatch. Convergence requires the maximum absolute power-balance residual to be below the requested tolerance, which defaults to 0.000001 in the network's power units. A nonfinite remaining mismatch is reported as not converged. Finite residuals are reported within relative 0.000001 plus absolute 0.0000000001 megawatts of the remaining mismatch.

- Generators default to prescribed active and reactive injection control. Generator controls determine bus controls: any voltage-controlled generator makes its bus voltage-controlled, while a slack generator takes precedence at its bus. Other buses retain prescribed active and reactive injections. Bus voltage targets default to one times nominal voltage. With exactly one slack generator per subnetwork, that generator provides the residual; if none is specified in a subnetwork containing generators, a generator is selected and its identity is exposed.

### FP-06: Economic dispatch and linear optimal power flow

- For a deterministic network with fixed capacities and a selected operating horizon, users can obtain least-cost dispatch under full foresight of that horizon. Each bus balances supply, demand and transfers at every snapshot. Generation obeys its lower and upper availability bounds, specified fixed dispatch and ramp limits. Flexible consumption can be represented by controllable negative injection whose costs express willingness to consume.

- Passive AC and DC branch flows obey linear electrical balance and the applicable branch ratings; AC flows depend on reactance and DC flows on resistance. Controllable transfers obey their specified transfer limits. Ordinary capacity optimisation retains the supplied branch impedances unless electrical expansion adjustment is explicitly requested.

- Operating cost includes linear cost per unit of dispatch and quadratic cost per squared unit of dispatch, multiplied by snapshot objective weights. Unspecified operating-cost coefficients are zero. An optimisation with no usable objective cost terms is refused as lacking an objective. A successfully solved problem exposes dispatch, branch flows, bus injections and objective value; infeasible termination is distinguishable from an optimal solution.

- Continuous optimal dispatch exposes nodal marginal prices and available constraint shadow prices. Prices represent the marginal objective effect of the corresponding balance or limit, with snapshot objective weighting removed from nodal energy prices.

- A transformer whose phase-shift lower bound is strictly below its upper bound has an optimised phase shift at each snapshot within those bounds. Otherwise it uses the supplied fixed phase shift. The realised phase shifts are available separately from the input phase shifts.

- Transmission is lossless by default. With passive-branch loss approximation, generation covers the represented losses and terminal power differs by those losses, with half the loss allocated to each terminal balance. The branch rating limits the sum of absolute through-flow and the full represented loss. Users can select an overestimate or underestimate of resistive loss as a function of through-flow; through-flow is halfway between sending-end input and receiving-end output. Resistive loss is effective resistance times squared through-flow on a common electrical base. For dispatch where additional losses strictly increase total operating cost, the upper approximation exceeds resistive loss by at most the larger of the supplied absolute tolerance in megawatts and relative tolerance times resistive loss; the lower approximation lies between zero and resistive loss. Under the same increasing-cost condition, the lower approximation's maximum shortfall over the admissible flow range is at most one quarter of the resistive loss at the branch's maximum possible flow divided by the square of the requested resolution. For the upper approximation, the supported accuracy scope covers branches whose maximum possible resistive loss is no greater than 1444 times the requested absolute tolerance. Upper tolerances default to one megawatt absolute and ten percent relative. Nonpositive absolute tolerance, negative relative tolerance and nonpositive lower resolution are refused; finite maximum passive-branch capacity is required.

- Users can add explicit load-shedding supply with a chosen penalty, so curtailed demand is quantified and penalised in the dispatch objective. Without such supply, demand remains a balance obligation.

- Generator, link and process dispatch bounds are fractions of nominal capacity, defaulting to zero below and one above; passive branch ratings limit the absolute flow in either direction and default to the full nominal rating. A fixed dispatch target imposes equality in addition to other bounds, so contradictory targets yield infeasibility. Positive generator dispatch supplies its bus and positive load demand withdraws from it; a supplied injection-sign multiplier reverses or scales that contribution. Assets marked inactive contribute zero dispatch.

- Users can bound the total generator-weighted dispatch of each generator over the selected snapshots from below or above. No cumulative dispatch bound applies unless supplied.

- For AC lines and transformers with positive effective reactance, a supplied finite maximum voltage-angle difference limits the absolute difference between terminal bus angles in both directions, including the transformer phase shift. The angle limit is measured in degrees and does not scale with installed capacity. Without a finite maximum there is no angle restriction.

- Upward and downward ramp limits bound changes between adjacent snapshots as fractions of nominal capacity; extendable noncommittable assets use their chosen capacity. These limits are per snapshot, not multiplied by elapsed hours. Without supplied ramp limits there is no ramp restriction. A supplied preceding dispatch constrains the first snapshot; without it the first snapshot has no ramp comparison. Ramps do not connect different investment periods.

### FP-07: Storage and sector coupling

- Storage units jointly constrain charging power, discharging power and stored energy; their energy capacity is nominal power multiplied by maximum discharge hours. Stores constrain energy independently of the power ratings of connected converters. Optimisation returns charging, discharging or net storage dispatch and the energy trajectory.

- Over each snapshot, energy carried from an earlier modelled snapshot first loses the specified fraction per hour, compounded over the storage elapsed hours. Supplied initial energy enters the first noncyclic energy balance without standing-loss attenuation. Charging adds elapsed hours of charging power multiplied by charging efficiency; discharging removes elapsed hours of discharging power divided by discharging efficiency. Storage units also add elapsed hours of inflow and subtract elapsed hours of spillage, which lies between zero and available inflow. Stores exchange their signed net power directly with stored energy. Ordinary marginal costs apply to storage-unit discharge and signed net store dispatch. Holding cost applies to end-of-snapshot energy and spillage cost to spillage power, both with objective snapshot weighting. Energy bounds and supplied energy setpoints restrict operation.

- Users can initialise storage with a prescribed energy level or require cyclic energy over the modelled horizon. With cyclic operation, final energy supplies the initial boundary and takes precedence over a separate initial-energy input. Multi-period storage can instead apply initial or cyclic boundaries independently within each investment period.

- Links convert an input carrier into one or more output carriers with specified, potentially time-varying efficiencies. Negative output efficiencies represent additional inputs consumed in fixed proportion to the primary input. Each connected bus receives the corresponding withdrawal or supply in its energy balance.

- Processes couple multiple ports through a common throughput with a supplied signed conversion rate at each port. Positive rates supply the connected bus and negative rates consume from it. 

- Links and processes can delay delivery at individual ports. For delays aligned with snapshot boundaries, delivery is displaced by the specified elapsed hours using generator snapshot weights. Cyclic delivery wraps within its investment period. Noncyclic delivery has no arrivals from before the horizon, and output whose delayed delivery would fall beyond the horizon is not delivered within it. Delays below zero or beyond a period's duration are identified by consistency checking and can be made strict failures.

- Storage initially contains zero energy, is noncyclic, has no standing loss or inflow, and has charging and discharging efficiencies of one. A storage unit defaults to one hour of energy capacity at nominal power and can charge and discharge up to that power; its stored energy is between zero and its energy capacity. A store defaults to energy bounds of zero and its nominal energy capacity. Users can fix storage-unit charging and discharging separately as well as its net dispatch.

- Link operating costs and nominal power refer to withdrawal at the primary input; process operating costs and nominal power refer to common throughput. Link output efficiencies default to one. A process defaults to consuming one unit at its first port and supplying one unit at its second port per unit throughput. Transport delay defaults to zero and cyclic delivery is the default.

### FP-08: Capacity expansion and investment pathways

- Users can jointly optimise operation and nominal capacities of generators, storage units, stores, links, processes, lines and transformers. Extendable capacity respects supplied lower and upper bounds or a specified fixed target. Investment can be continuous or restricted to whole multiples of a supplied module size. Existing nominal capacity is not an implicit lower bound for an extendable asset.

- Investment cost and operating cost jointly determine the selected capacities. Periodised capital cost applies per unit of chosen capacity. Alternatively, overnight investment cost is converted to equal annual repayments over the supplied lifetime at the supplied discount rate and scaled by represented years, measured as objective-weighted hours divided by 8760; periodised fixed operation and maintenance cost is added. Overnight cost takes precedence over a simultaneously supplied periodised capital cost.

- At zero discount rate, positive finite-lifetime overnight cost is spread equally over the lifetime. Direct financing calculations for a single asset refuse nonpositive finite lifetimes. Overnight-cost planning supports positive finite lifetimes. For infinite lifetime, a positive discount rate gives annual financing cost at that rate and a nonpositive rate gives zero annualised overnight cost. Overnight-cost planning with unequal represented durations across investment periods is refused.

- Multiple investment periods are co-optimised with shared decisions about assets and period-specific operation. An asset is active from its build year inclusive until build year plus lifetime exclusive; its inactive-period dispatch is zero. Capacity is fixed or extendable according to the caller's choice, including for assets built before the first investment period. Objective period weights scale active-period cost, while represented-year weights scale cumulative resource budgets.

- By default, the reported objective deducts the periodised investment and fixed operation and maintenance cost of the supplied nominal capacity of active extendable assets. Users can disable this deduction. Users can fix optimised capacities for subsequent operational studies.

- In transmission expansion with electrical adjustment enabled, added parallel line capacity reduces electrical impedance in inverse proportion to capacity, and the resulting operating solution uses the adjusted network. For AC lines the adjusted electrical quantity is reactance; for DC lines it is resistance. Users can request discrete line and link capacity increments and obtain operational results for the resulting fixed capacities. Except near a nonintegral upper bound, the final capacity rounds up when its fractional increment reaches a caller-supplied threshold and down otherwise; the threshold defaults to thirty percent. If the upper bound is smaller than one increment it is used directly. For a nonintegral upper bound less than one increment above the continuous solution, the result is the lower whole increment unless a fractional final increment is allowed and its fractional completion reaches the threshold, in which case the upper bound is used.

- Extendable capacity defaults to a lower bound of zero and no finite upper bound. Without a positive module size investment is continuous. Unspecified capital and fixed operation and maintenance costs are zero; lifetime is unlimited and build year is zero. A fixed capacity target is imposed together with its bounds and module size, and incompatible choices make the problem infeasible.

### FP-09: Unit commitment and maintenance scheduling

- Generators, links and processes can have on/off commitment. A stopped asset has zero dispatch; a running asset obeys its committed minimum and maximum output. Start and stop events, minimum up and down durations measured in snapshots, prior operating history and startup, shutdown and ordinary ramp limits constrain the schedule. Results expose status, starts and stops alongside dispatch.

- Startup and shutdown costs are charged per event without snapshot objective weighting. Standby costs apply while committed and are weighted by snapshot objective weights. Start and stop indicators cover transitions; when their costs and minimum-duration constraints do not select a unique schedule, redundant event indicators may also be present.

- Commitment can accompany capacity expansion, including expansion without a supplied finite maximum. For modular capacity, commitment can represent an integer count of operating modules within the installed count. Users can instead request relaxed commitment with fractional status between zero and one; combining this relaxation with modular commitment is refused.

- Maintainable generators, links and processes receive exactly the requested number of non-overlapping contiguous maintenance events within their active operating horizon. Maintenance elapsed hours are measured by generator snapshot weights. Each event covers at least its requested elapsed hours, rounded up to a snapshot boundary with overshoot smaller than the last covered snapshot's duration. An event cannot start where its duration would extend beyond the active horizon.

- During maintenance, available capacity is reduced by the specified fraction; the default fraction is one, meaning complete unavailability. Maintenance may be combined with commitment and capacity expansion, retaining both the operating-status restriction and the maintenance capacity reduction. Results distinguish maintenance starts and periods under maintenance from ordinary shutdowns.

- Maintenance with nonpositive duration or event count, or total requested maintenance time exceeding the horizon, is refused. Maintainable extendable assets require finite maximum capacity and are refused without it.

- Minimum up and down durations default to zero snapshots. Prior history defaults to one snapshot online and zero offline; a positive supplied offline history takes precedence over an online history. Supplied history determines any remaining minimum-duration obligation at the start. When continuing an already solved sequence, preceding solved statuses determine this history. Minimum-duration requirements at the end cover only the remaining snapshots of the selected horizon. Start and stop costs and standby cost default to zero. A startup ramp limits output in the starting snapshot; a shutdown ramp limits output in the last running snapshot. Maintenance defaults to one event and requires a positive duration supplied by the caller.

### FP-10: System-wide resource and policy constraints

- Users can constrain primary-energy use or carrier-associated emissions over the selected horizon, with an upper bound, lower bound or equality. Generator output is divided by its efficiency and multiplied by its carrier coefficient and generator snapshot weight. For noncyclic storage, initial energy less final energy contributes the carrier coefficient times that depletion. Cyclic storage contributes no net depletion.

- Users can bound carrier-specific operational production: generator-weighted generator dispatch plus noncyclic storage depletion, without efficiency or emission coefficients. Transmission volume limits sum nominal capacity times length over extendable lines and links of the selected carriers; transmission investment limits sum their chosen capacity times investment cost. Technology capacity limits sum extendable capacity selected by carrier, optionally restricted to a bus and investment period. All three comparisons, upper bound, lower bound and equality, are supported. Contradictory imposed limits yield infeasibility; continuous solutions expose available marginal effects of these limits.

- Multi-period planning can restrict newly activated extendable capacity of each carrier using a finite absolute allowance and an optional nonnegative relative allowance multiplied by capacity newly activated in the preceding investment period. Both allowances contribute to the permitted addition; the first period has only the absolute allowance. Capacity is counted in its first active modelled period. Constraints scoped to an investment period apply only to assets active in that period; specifying such a scope without multi-period optimisation is refused.

### FP-11: Custom optimisation models and aggregate expressions

- Users can obtain an editable optimisation model of their network, introduce decision quantities and constraints, and replace or extend its objective. Solving the modified model respects these additions and writes the resulting network decisions back to the network. Contradictory user constraints lead to infeasible termination rather than an unconstrained network result.

- Users can express aggregate capacity, capital expenditure, operating expenditure, operation, energy balance, supply, withdrawal, curtailment and transmission as quantities in the optimisation problem, selecting and grouping components by carrier, bus and port. Capacity uses optimised nominal capacity; capital expenditure is capacity multiplied by capital cost. Operation and energy use generator snapshot weights, while ordinary linear operating expenditure uses objective snapshot weights. Energy balance is signed bus contribution; supply and withdrawal are positive directional contributions for unidirectional flows; curtailment is available generation less dispatch; transmission is the signed bus-side contribution of branches joining buses of the same carrier. Constraints and objectives built from these quantities affect the same decisions represented in the returned network results.

### FP-12: Near-optimal alternative plans

- Starting from a solved cost-optimal deterministic single-period network with ordinary linear investment and operating costs, no separate fixed operation and maintenance cost, and zero installed investment cost, users can minimise or maximise a weighted combination of selected investment quantities or selected operational quantities, without mixing these two dimensions in one objective, subject to the original network constraints and a total-system-cost allowance. The allowance is the original optimal investment plus operating cost increased by the supplied relative slack, which defaults to five percent. The resulting alternative exposes its decisions, alternative objective and cost restriction. Reported alternative objectives use the minimisation sign: a maximised quantity is reported with its sign reversed. Linear alternative-plan quantities, feasibility residuals, objectives and budget residuals are accurate within absolute 0.00001 plus relative 0.000001.

- Users can define named aggregate decision dimensions and maximise their weighted combination in one or more requested directions. A single-direction result includes its coordinates and corresponding feasible network solution under the cost allowance. A multiple-direction result includes direction and coordinate tables for each feasible alternative, without a corresponding network solution for each row.

- Requesting alternatives without a solved reference network is refused as missing the baseline optimum. An unrecognised minimisation or maximisation choice is refused. Direction specifications must identify the same dimensions as the requested projection; inconsistent dimensions are refused.

### FP-13: Piecewise costs and conversion performance

- Users can replace ordinary operating-cost relationships with piecewise cost curves for generators, links, processes, storage units and stores, and capital-cost relationships with piecewise curves for those assets and for lines and transformers. Optimisation selects dispatch and investment subject to the supplied curves and exposes realised costs per unit of dispatch or capacity for generators, links, processes, storage units and stores. For lines and transformers it exposes capacity and the resulting objective.

- Cumulative cost curves start at zero quantity and price each quantity interval at the marginal value attached to its upper breakpoint, accumulating total cost from zero. The marginal value at zero prices no quantity. Cumulative interpretation is used for optimisation costs.

- Piecewise generator efficiencies determine fuel requirements; piecewise link efficiencies and process conversion rates determine port supply or withdrawal. At each positive-output breakpoint, generator fuel is output divided by efficiency; zero output requires zero fuel even when efficiency at zero is zero. Link and process port quantities follow their efficiency or signed rate at that throughput. Between breakpoints, fuel or port quantity varies linearly with throughput.

- Per-unit operating curves scale their quantity axis by fixed nominal capacity. These curves are refused for extendable assets and for zero, missing or infinite nominal capacity. Absolute-capacity cost curves can apply to extendable assets.

- Curves must begin at zero quantity; per-unit operating curves with an endpoint below one unit of nominal capacity are refused. A link-efficiency or process-rate curve containing both positive and negative values is refused as unsupported. Piecewise capital cost combined with overnight investment cost for the same asset is refused as conflicting cost descriptions.

- By default, realised piecewise operating and capital costs cannot be below their curve values and enter the cost-minimising objective with their applicable time and period weights. Users can instead require realised costs to equal the curve values. For storage units, the operating-cost curve is evaluated on discharging power.

### FP-14: Stochastic and risk-averse planning

- Two-stage stochastic planning chooses investment capacities shared across scenarios and scenario-specific operation. Each scenario satisfies its own availability, demand and operational constraints on the common network topology. Risk-neutral planning minimises investment cost plus probability-weighted operating cost, replacing the single deterministic future used in ordinary planning.

- Users can add risk aversion based on conditional value at risk of operating cost. At the supplied confidence level, tail cost is the probability-weighted mean of the highest-cost scenario outcomes occupying the remaining probability mass; the boundary scenario contributes only the fraction needed to fill that mass. The objective blends expected operating cost and tail operating cost by the supplied risk weight, together with investment cost.

- Confidence must be strictly between zero and one and risk weight between zero and one inclusive; values outside those ranges are refused. Risk aversion without scenarios and risk aversion combined with quadratic operating costs are refused as unsupported.

### FP-15: Branch outages and security-constrained dispatch

- For non-islanding outages of individual passive branches in an all-active network at one selected snapshot, users can obtain the base-case linear flows and the redistributed flows after each specified outage. The outaged branch carries no flow in its outage case and surviving branches carry the redistributed injections. Independently calculated powers and costs are accurate within absolute 0.00001 plus relative 0.000001; branch ratings may be exceeded by at most 0.00001 in network power units.

- Security-constrained optimisation chooses dispatch satisfying ordinary network constraints and surviving-branch ratings under every requested individual passive-branch outage. Each outage uses the topology and asset activity of its investment period. The resulting base-case dispatch and objective are available as network results.

- With no explicit outage selection, all passive branches active in the optimised periods are considered; for single-period security-constrained optimisation this capability covers networks whose passive branches are all active. Security-constrained optimisation refuses explicitly selected branches absent or inactive in the optimised periods. Outage-flow analysis refuses absent selected branches as missing identities. Outage analysis here applies to outages that leave the affected subnetwork connected.

### FP-16: Rolling horizon operation

- Users can optimise an operating horizon with a specified lookahead window and overlap measured in snapshots. Decisions outside the current lookahead do not influence its objective. Successive windows produce a dispatch trajectory over the requested horizon, refining ordinary full-horizon foresight to the chosen limited foresight. Compared dispatch and carried-state quantities are accurate within absolute 0.00001 plus relative 0.000001.

- The retained trajectory carries storage energy, preceding dispatch for ramp limits, and commitment history into subsequent windows. Overlapping snapshots can be reconsidered with the next window's information while retained earlier decisions provide its initial conditions.

- An overlap greater than or equal to the lookahead window is refused. When maintenance is included, the requested event count applies separately to each window, and an event in progress is not continued across a window boundary.

### FP-17: Spatial network aggregation

- Users can supply a mapping from original buses to aggregate buses and obtain a smaller network plus mappings of the original buses and lines. One-port components are reassigned to aggregate buses, all ports of retained links are remapped, internal lines are removed, and lines between the same pair of aggregate buses are combined. Links whose primary input and first output become the same bus are removed. This aggregation covers buses, lines, links and one-port assets.

- Selected one-port assets are combined within their component category, aggregate bus and carrier. Nominal capacity, capacity bounds, aggregation weights, active and reactive injections, their prescribed targets and inflow are summed. Actual active and reactive power series are aggregated when every member has a populated series for that output. Availability bounds, marginal cost, capital cost, efficiency and storage duration use nominal-capacity-weighted means, with equal weights if the group has no positive total nominal capacity. Ramp limits use arithmetic means. Aggregated assets have build year zero, unlimited lifetime and no retained power-flow control designation. Users can select which carriers to combine and replace attribute aggregation rules. Other attributes must agree within each group or aggregation is refused.

- Users can request a positive number of bus groups from geographical coordinates with positive integer bus weights, from supplied bus features with network connectivity, or from network connection strength. For geographical grouping with at least that many distinct weighted locations, and feature-based grouping with at least that many selected buses, the requested number of nonempty groups is returned. Each selected bus belongs to exactly one group. Geographical groups are separable into nearest-centre regions in coordinate space: there is a distinct representative location for each group such that every assigned bus is closest to its group's location, allowing equal-distance ties. Buses at identical coordinates belong to the same group. The grouping can be used to produce an aggregate network.

- Spatial aggregation of a stochastic network is refused as unsupported.

- Aggregate bus coordinates are arithmetic means, nominal voltage is the largest in the group, and voltage bounds are their intersection. Combined line capacities and capacity bounds are summed; availability bounds are capacity-weighted means, using equal weights when total capacity is zero. Parallel-circuit counts are summed, terrain factors use arithmetic means, build years use capacity-weighted means converted to integer years, lifetimes use capacity-weighted means, and angle bounds use their intersection. A combined line is extendable if any member is extendable. Users can supply additional line grouping attributes so only lines agreeing on those attributes are combined.

- For longitude and latitude coordinates in degrees, combined line length is the great-circle distance on a sphere of radius 6371 kilometres multiplied by a caller-supplied factor, which defaults to one. For positive original lengths and voltages, each member line is represented at that length and the greater aggregate terminal voltage: its series resistance and reactance correspond to the length ratio and the squared ratio of aggregate to original first-terminal nominal voltage, and its shunt conductance and susceptance correspond to the length ratio and the inverse squared voltage ratio. The reciprocal of the combined resistance is the sum of reciprocals of the adjusted member resistances, and the same rule applies independently to reactance. Adjusted shunt conductances and susceptances add. Combined capital cost is the capacity-weighted mean of each line's cost scaled by the length ratio. Retained links with positive original length receive the same geographic length adjustment, with proportional capital-cost adjustment enabled by default; links without positive original length keep zero length and their capital cost.

- With default feature-based grouping on a connected selected network, each group is connected through the selected branch categories. If identical feature vectors define exactly the requested number of connected regions, with a different vector for each region, those regions are the returned groups. Connection-strength grouping of a connected line-only network with positive finite line capacities and nonzero impedances returns the requested number of nonempty groups, each connected by original lines.

### FP-18: Temporal network aggregation

- Users can resample timestamp snapshots, retain snapshots at a chosen stride, supply a mapping from snapshots to representative snapshots, or request contiguous time-series segmentation. The resulting network retains component static data and returns the correspondence between original and aggregated snapshots with their represented durations.

- Resampling and caller-mapped aggregation sum snapshot weights within each time group and use arithmetic mean time-varying values by default. Energy upper bounds use the group's minimum and energy lower bounds use its maximum. User-supplied aggregation rules replace the defaults for selected attributes.

- After temporal aggregation, for an asset whose paired lower and upper availability or energy bounds both have time series, a lower bound exceeding its paired upper bound is reduced to that upper bound.

- For weights uniform within each investment period, stride downsampling retains the first snapshot of each consecutive stride-sized group and its time-series values, with weights representing all snapshots in that group, including a shorter final group. A stride less than one is refused.

- A supplied snapshot mapping must have the same ordered source snapshots as the network; a mismatch is refused. Resampling and downsampling operate separately within investment periods rather than combining observations from different periods.

- For a single-period network with unit-hour snapshot weights and nonempty time-varying data, segmentation returns the requested number of contiguous representative segments and their durations, preserving total represented hours; a segment count below one or greater than the number of snapshots is refused. Segmentation of multiple investment periods and temporal aggregation of stochastic networks are refused. Segmentation without time-varying data is refused; segmentation without its required optional support is refused as unavailable.

- For fixed whole-hour or whole-day resampling intervals on timestamp snapshots, time groups begin at midnight on the first represented day and then at successive interval boundaries. Each group includes its starting boundary and excludes the next boundary; a final partially populated group retains the snapshots present in it. Each investment period uses its own first represented day.

- For segmentation of timestamp snapshots with unit-hour weights and finite time-series values, each included attribute's representative value is the arithmetic mean of that attribute over the original snapshots assigned to the segment, subject to the paired-bound correction. The representative timestamp is the first original timestamp in that segment. Energy lower-bound series are excluded from segmentation by default; callers can change which attributes are excluded. Excluded attributes retain their values at representative timestamps.

### FP-19: System statistics and network comparison

- Statistics can be filtered and grouped by component category, carrier, bus, bus carrier and port. Built-in grouping also covers bus country, bus location, individual asset name and unit; callers can group by other supplied component attributes or a user-defined grouping. Results retain unaggregated snapshots or provide time sums or weighted means, and retain investment periods and uncertainty scenarios as separate dimensions when requested.

- Energy totals apply generator snapshot weights to power. Capacity factor is generator-weighted mean absolute operation divided by optimised capacity: operation is generator dispatch, storage-unit net discharge, store energy, link input, process throughput or first-terminal passive-branch flow. Curtailment is available generation less realised generation, bounded below by zero. Revenue is net supply valued at connected-bus marginal prices using objective snapshot weights. With time-sum aggregation, market value is revenue divided by generator-weighted absolute operation; for a multiport branch, its denominator uses the primary port. Default time aggregation computes the ratio of separately weighted means.

- Investment statistics apply asset activity to each investment period.

- Users can compare a labeled collection of networks whose component, snapshot, period and scenario dimensions align. Combined data and statistics retain network labels, support label-based selections and grouping, and preserve each network's scenario dimension. Changes to a member network are visible through the collection. A label count different from the number of networks is refused.

- Supply sums positive injections at the selected ports; withdrawal sums the magnitudes of negative injections; energy balance retains their sign. Installed capacity is supplied nominal capacity and optimal capacity is solved nominal capacity. Port-specific capacity uses the static conversion coefficient, with signs indicating consumption or production; when only the primary port is requested, its coefficient magnitude is used. Expanded capacity is optimal less installed capacity where optimal absolute capacity exceeds installed absolute capacity, otherwise zero. Storage-unit energy capacity multiplies power capacity by maximum discharge hours.

- For ordinary investment costs, capital expenditure multiplies nominal capacity by annualised investment cost excluding fixed operation and maintenance cost; installed and optimal expenditure use their respective capacities. Expanded capital expenditure is optimal expenditure less installed expenditure and can be negative. Overnight investment expenditure uses overnight cost per unit optimal capacity; fixed operation and maintenance expenditure uses its separate coefficient. System cost adds investment, fixed operation and maintenance and operating expenditure. Ordinary operating expenditure includes dispatch, quadratic dispatch, stored-energy holding, spillage, startup, shutdown and standby contributions when supplied. Objective period weights scale capital, fixed operation and maintenance, and operating expenditure in multi-period results.

- Marginal-price summaries use objective snapshot weights. Users can choose time-weighted means or weight additionally by total withdrawal at each bus, the default; for buses with positive withdrawal the latter is total withdrawal-valued price divided by withdrawal. Grouped prices use the same weights across the selected buses. Transmission statistics report signed flow at selected ports of branches joining buses of the same carrier, applying generator weights for energy totals.

- Statistics group by carrier and sum components within each component category by default; users can retain individual assets or combine categories. Time aggregation defaults to weighted totals for energy and operating expenditure, and to weighted means for utilisation and prices. Without a bus-carrier filter, the default port is the primary port; a bus-carrier filter considers all connected ports unless ports are explicitly selected. Zero-valued aggregate entries are omitted by default and can be retained.

### FP-20: Geographic and statistical visualisation

- Users can produce static and interactive geographic views of buses, lines, transformers and links at their supplied locations, with selected component quantities controlling bus marker area, branch widths and colours. For bus quantities, marker area is proportional to the supplied nonnegative quantity; a zero quantity has zero area. Bus composition can be displayed as carrier sectors whose angular shares are proportional to each carrier's share of the bus total, and branch flows can display direction. Selecting a snapshot displays that snapshot's quantities.

- Users can visualise network and collection statistics as area, bar, map, scatter, line, box, violin and histogram charts. Collection statistics can be shown in these chart families except maps. In statistical maps, marker area is proportional to the represented capacity. Selected metrics, groups and filters determine the displayed values; stacking and facets separate the requested categories. Interactive statistical charts support area, bar and line. Static and interactive views retain the associated labels and units.

- Supplied carrier colours and display names affect statistical chart categories and legends; colours also affect static statistical maps. Statistical area, bar, scatter, line, box, violin and histogram charts require carrier colours and refuse missing colours. A requested strict plotting consistency check also refuses missing carrier colours.
