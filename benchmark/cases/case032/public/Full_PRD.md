# Probabilistic Modeling Product Requirements

This probabilistic programming library supports expressing statistical models, working with probability distributions, performing Bayesian inference, and examining posterior results.

### FP-01: Probability distributions

- Callers can specify a probability law and its numerical parameters, obtain random draws on its support, and evaluate the log probability mass or log density of supplied values. Repeating the same sampling request with the same random key gives the same draws; separate random keys permit independent replications of the law.

- The continuous scalar families are normal, Cauchy, half normal, half Cauchy, Student t, Laplace, asymmetric Laplace, soft Laplace, logistic, uniform, log uniform, log normal, exponential, gamma, inverse gamma, chi-squared, beta, Kumaraswamy, Pareto, Weibull, Gumbel, Gompertz, Lévy, and Dagum. Their probability laws use the supplied location, scale, shape, concentration, rate, bounds, or quantile specification. Normal scale is standard deviation; log-normal location and scale describe the logarithm. Gamma uses a shape and rate, and inverse gamma describes the reciprocal of that gamma variable. Half normal and half Cauchy are absolute values of their zero-location base laws. Soft Laplace is the hyperbolic secant law with the supplied location and standard deviation equal to pi divided by two times its supplied scale. When a listed law permits omission of location or scale, their defaults are zero and one respectively. Omitted uniform lower and upper bounds are zero and one respectively.

- The discrete families are Bernoulli, binomial, categorical, multinomial, Poisson, geometric, discrete uniform, negative binomial, beta-binomial, beta-negative-binomial, Dirichlet-multinomial, gamma-Poisson, and ordered logistic. Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, and negative binomial accept probabilities or log odds; categorical and multinomial laws accept probabilities or unnormalized log weights. Categorical outcomes are zero-based category indices, and multinomial outcomes are nonnegative count vectors totaling the trial count. Binomial counts successes, and geometric counts failures before the first success. The negative-binomial count parameter may be any positive real. Negative binomial is the gamma-Poisson mixture with concentration equal to the supplied count and gamma rate equal to the reciprocal of the supplied probability minus one; its mean-and-concentration form instead has gamma rate equal to concentration divided by mean. Beta also accepts mean and concentration, whose two beta concentrations are the mean and its complement multiplied by the total concentration. Beta-binomial and beta-negative-binomial integrate the corresponding probability-parameterized count law over a beta-distributed probability with the supplied two concentrations; Dirichlet-multinomial integrates multinomial probabilities over the supplied Dirichlet law. Gamma-Poisson integrates a Poisson rate over the supplied shape-and-rate gamma law. Discrete uniform includes both integer bounds.

- The vector, matrix, spatial, and sequential families are Dirichlet, multivariate normal, multivariate Student t, matrix normal, Wishart, inverse Wishart, LKJ correlation, conditional autoregressive normal, circulant normal, Gaussian random walk, Gaussian state space, zero-sum normal, and a stochastic process with Euler–Maruyama transition laws. Callers can specify covariance, precision, or a covariance Cholesky factor for a multivariate normal; a low-rank form accepts a covariance equal to a supplied rectangular factor times its transpose plus a positive supplied diagonal; Wishart, inverse Wishart, and LKJ also have distributions over their Cholesky factors. Matrix normal uses supplied row and column covariance factors. Gaussian random walks have independent zero-mean normal increments with the supplied scale, starting from zero, and return the requested number of accumulated increments.

- The directional families are von Mises, sine bivariate von Mises, projected normal, and sine-skewed distributions. Von Mises uses a circular location and positive concentration. With sample validation disabled, its density is periodic over a full turn; with validation enabled, the circular support check takes precedence. Projected normal is the directional law of an isotropic unit-covariance normal vector with the supplied mean; random draws support arbitrary vector dimensions, while density evaluation is limited to two and three dimensions and other dimensions are refused as unsupported. Sine bivariate von Mises accepts two angular locations, two concentrations, and either their sine-interaction correlation or a correlation weight multiplied by the square root of the concentration product. Sine skewing applies to a toroidal base law symmetric about its supplied center, with skew weights whose absolute values total at most one; its density reweights the base density by one plus the weighted sum of the sines of the centered angles. A relaxed Bernoulli supplies a continuous probability-valued relaxation at the caller’s positive temperature, and a point mass returns the supplied point with its supplied log weight.

- Sample dimensions precede distribution batch dimensions and event dimensions. Numerical parameters broadcast across compatible batch dimensions; evaluating a joint event removes its event dimensions from the returned log probabilities. Expanding a batch preserves the law of each entry, while reinterpreting trailing batch dimensions as one event sums their log probabilities. Incompatible batch expansion and a requested event rank greater than the available batch rank are refused as incompatible shapes.

- For normal, uniform, exponential, Cauchy, half Cauchy, logistic, Laplace, and beta laws, cumulative probabilities and inverse cumulative probabilities describe the same law on its interior. Weibull laws provide cumulative probabilities on their interior. Analytic Kullback–Leibler divergence is available between two normal laws, two full-covariance multivariate normal laws, two Dirichlet laws, two beta laws, two gamma laws, two probability-parameterized categorical laws, and a Weibull law relative to a gamma law; a pair lacking a defined divergence is refused as an unsupported operation.

- With argument validation enabled for concrete numerical inputs, scalar scale, rate, concentration, shape, asymmetry, temperature, degrees-of-freedom, probability, and trial-count parameters that violate the following domains are refused as invalid parameters, subject to the exceptions below. Scales, concentrations, positive-shape parameters, asymmetry, relaxation temperature, and scalar degrees of freedom must be positive. Rates are positive except that a Poisson rate may be zero, giving a point mass at zero; a negative-binomial mean may also be zero, giving zero counts. Probability inputs lie between zero and one, probability vectors total one, and discrete trial counts are nonnegative integers. Categorical log weights may contain negative infinity but must contain a finite entry and may contain neither undefined numbers nor positive infinity. A valid geometric law has success probability greater than zero and at most one, but refusal of zero success probability is not guaranteed. Negative-binomial probability is at least zero and less than one. Parameter-refusal guarantees exclude relaxed Bernoulli probability inputs and dimension-dependent degrees-of-freedom bounds for Wishart and inverse Wishart laws, including their factor forms; their valid mathematical domains still apply. With sample validation enabled, the listed laws other than projected normal and sine-skewed warn on values outside their support and return negative infinite log probability there. These validation guarantees concern directly evaluated numerical inputs.

- Distribution values and parameters can be passed into and returned from compiled or vectorized numerical computations while retaining their numerical laws, batches, and events. For a collection of distributions returned by vectorized construction, these guarantees apply when each member is evaluated through the corresponding vectorized computation; each member retains its own batch and event dimensions. The collection itself need not behave as a distribution with a combined batch. Log densities are differentiable with respect to continuous parameters at interior differentiable points; normal, gamma, beta, and Student t draws additionally support derivatives of differentiable expectations with respect to their continuous parameters. For finite first- and second-moment derivatives at well-conditioned interior parameters with finite estimator variance, the mean derivative estimate across independent sampling replications agrees with the mathematical expectation derivative, with high probability, within 0.005 plus twelve estimated standard errors. A standard error is the sample standard deviation of the replicate derivative estimates divided by the square root of the number of replications.

- Asymmetric Laplace has a left exponential scale equal to the supplied scale times asymmetry and a right exponential scale equal to scale divided by asymmetry, with a common density at the location. Its quantile-parameterized form places the supplied probability below the location, with left scale equal to the supplied scale divided by the complement of that probability and right scale equal to scale divided by that probability. The quantile must be strictly between zero and one.

- Gaussian state-space paths use a caller-supplied fixed square transition matrix and independent zero-mean multivariate normal innovations with a supplied covariance, precision, or covariance factor. Each state has conditional mean equal to the transition matrix applied to the preceding state and the supplied innovation covariance. The caller supplies an initial state, defaulting to zero; the returned path contains the requested positive number of subsequent states and excludes that initial state. Zero-sum normal is the centered isotropic normal law restricted to zero sum along every designated event axis; its supplied scale is the standard deviation before the zero-sum restriction.

- For a supplied increasing time grid, initial probability law, and drift and diffusion functions, the Euler–Maruyama path includes the initial draw and one value for each subsequent grid time. A transition’s conditional mean is the preceding value plus the elapsed time times its drift at the preceding time and value; conditional variance is elapsed time times squared diffusion, independently for each coordinate. Circulant normal uses a supplied mean and a positive-definite circulant covariance specified by its first row or its nonredundant Fourier spectrum.

- Callers can enumerate every supported value of Bernoulli, categorical, ordered logistic, binomial, beta-binomial, and discrete uniform laws, optionally repeating the enumeration across the law’s batch. Binomial and beta-binomial enumeration requires a common trial count across the batch; discrete uniform enumeration requires common lower and upper bounds. Requests to enumerate those laws with differing batch support sizes or bounds are refused as unsupported enumeration.

- Conditional autoregressive normal accepts a supplied mean vector, correlation strictly between negative one and one, positive conditional-precision multiplier, and a symmetric nonnegative adjacency matrix with zero diagonal and positive row sums. Its joint precision is the multiplier times the difference between the diagonal matrix of row sums and the correlation-weighted adjacency matrix. Wishart and inverse Wishart degrees of freedom must exceed the matrix dimension minus one, and LKJ correlation matrices have dimension at least two and a positive concentration; omitted LKJ concentration is one.

- Ordered logistic accepts a real predictor and strictly increasing cutpoints and returns zero-based ordered categories. The cumulative probability through a category is the logistic sigmoid of its cutpoint minus the predictor; the final category receives the remaining probability. There is one more category than cutpoints. With argument validation enabled, cutpoints that are not strictly increasing are refused as invalid ordinal thresholds.

- Callers can obtain population means and marginal variances, when mathematically finite, for normal, half normal, Student t, Laplace, asymmetric Laplace in either parameterization, soft Laplace, logistic, uniform, log uniform, log normal, exponential, gamma, inverse gamma, chi-squared, beta in either parameterization, Kumaraswamy, Pareto, Weibull, Gumbel, and Dagum laws; for Bernoulli, binomial, multinomial, Poisson, geometric, discrete uniform, negative binomial, beta-binomial, beta-negative-binomial, Dirichlet-multinomial, and gamma-Poisson laws; and for Dirichlet, multivariate normal including its low-rank form, multivariate Student t, Gaussian random walk, Gaussian state space, circulant normal, zero-sum normal, Wishart, inverse Wishart, and Wishart Cholesky-factor laws. Returned moments are broadcastable to each law’s batch and event shape.

- Analytic entropy is available for normal, Student t, Laplace, logistic, uniform, log uniform, log normal, exponential, gamma, inverse gamma, beta, Dirichlet, Pareto, Weibull, Cauchy, Lévy, multivariate normal including its low-rank form, circulant normal, Wishart, Bernoulli, categorical, discrete uniform, and geometric laws. Continuous laws return differential entropy; discrete laws return probability-mass entropy.

- Across the library's capabilities, finite deterministic numerical results other than initial potential derivatives must agree with their mathematical values to an absolute error no greater than 0.000002 plus 0.00002 times the magnitude of the mathematical value, per scalar result or array element. This covers log probabilities, cumulative probabilities and quantiles, analytic moments, entropies and divergences, differentiable probability derivatives, transformed and recovered coordinates, log probability-volume changes, model arithmetic, objective evaluation at supplied random values, numerical optimizer updates, posterior summaries and diagnostics computed from supplied draws, neural outputs at supplied parameters, particle similarities, and finite Gaussian-process basis and spectral evaluations. The accuracy applies with double-precision numerical evaluation at interior, well-conditioned inputs, away from singular matrices, unresolved probability differences, and extreme tails; it does not prescribe the numerical method or a particular reversible mapping. It concerns numerical evaluation conditional on supplied or returned values, separately from sampling variation, posterior approximation error, and optimization convergence.

### FP-02: Support constraints and probability transformations

- Callers can classify supplied values against boolean, real scalar, real vector, real matrix, complex, extended real, positive, nonnegative, bounded real interval, open real interval, one-sided real bound, bounded integer interval, integer lower bound, positive integer, nonnegative integer, probability simplex, categorical log-weight, multinomial count, ordered vector, positive ordered vector, circular, unit sphere, unit L1 ball, lower Cholesky, correlation Cholesky, correlation matrix, positive definite matrix, positive semidefinite matrix, scaled unit lower Cholesky, and zero-sum domains. Real scalar, real vector, and real matrix domains require finite values; extended real additionally allows both infinities but excludes undefined numbers. One-sided real-bound and ordered-domain guarantees concern finite inputs and rejection of undefined numbers. Closed real and integer intervals include their endpoints; open intervals and strict one-sided bounds exclude them, and an inclusive lower-bound form is also available. Integer lower bounds are inclusive. Circular values lie between negative pi and pi, including endpoints. Simplex entries are nonnegative and total one. For caller-supplied integer-valued vectors, multinomial membership requires nonnegativity and equality of the total to the supplied count; fractional-input classification is outside this guarantee. Ordered vectors are strictly increasing; a positive ordered vector additionally starts above zero. A unit L1 ball bounds the sum of absolute coordinates by one, and zero-sum events sum to zero along each designated event axis. Membership is reported per batch entry after accounting for the domain’s event dimensions; categorical log-weights exclude undefined numbers, positive infinity, and vectors containing no finite entry.

- Callers can obtain a feasible value with the supplied batch and event shape for a concrete domain, combine domain checks over consecutive slices, and treat designated trailing dimensions as a single constrained event. A dependent domain whose bounds have not been specified cannot provide a concrete membership decision and is refused as an unspecified constraint.

- Callers can apply identity, absolute-value, affine, exponential, power, logistic sigmoid, softplus, ordered-vector, real-vector-to-simplex, simplex-to-ordered, unit L1 ball, Cholesky, correlation Cholesky, correlation-matrix Cholesky, lower Cholesky, softplus lower Cholesky, scaled unit lower Cholesky, lower Cholesky affine, zero-sum, permutation, reshape, real-to-complex, recursive linear, Haar, type-II discrete cosine, real Fourier transformations, and reversible packing between real coordinates and the nonredundant coefficients of a real Fourier transform. Transformations report their input and output domains and event shapes. Invertible transformations recover the original interior value from its transformed value and provide the log absolute volume change for probability evaluation. For simplex events, volume uses all but the final coordinate; for matrix and factor events it uses the independent lower triangle, excluding the diagonal for correlations. Zero-sum embeddings use induced Euclidean volume. Default Haar and discrete cosine transformations and real Fourier coefficient packing have unit absolute volume; other spectral normalizations report the volume of their actual mapping. Real Fourier inversion is promised when the transformed axis lengths equal the input lengths and the original lengths are supplied; transformations that discard input elements do not promise recovery of discarded values.

- Callers can compose transformations in a supplied order, apply separate transformations to consecutive slices, aggregate volume changes over event dimensions, and pack or unpack structured values using caller-supplied mappings. A reshape that changes the number of event elements is refused as an incompatible shape; inversion without a supplied inverse for a structured mapping is refused as unsupported.

- For positive scalars, bounded real intervals, ordered vectors, probability simplexes, correlation matrices and their Cholesky factors, positive definite matrices, lower Cholesky factors, unit L1 balls, and zero-sum events, callers can request a reversible mapping between unconstrained coordinates and the interior of the domain. A domain without an available reversible mapping is refused as unsupported.

- Callers can parameterize inverse autoregressive and block neural autoregressive probability transformations with compatible neural network outputs. Draws transformed by these mappings have the corresponding change-of-variables density, and their reported volume change is differentiable with respect to the supplied trainable numerical inputs. For an inverse autoregressive mapping, each output has the location and positive multiplicative scale supplied by its corresponding network outputs; the logarithm of that scale is confined to caller-selected bounds, defaulting to negative five through three. Values beyond a bound produce the same transformed value as that bound. The block neural form exposes forward evaluation and volume changes; an inverse is not promised.

- Callers can construct autoregressive neural mappings with a supplied input ordering and output multiplicities. Output coordinates depend only on their permitted preceding input coordinates. The mappings can express nonconstant dependence on permitted preceding coordinates with supplied trainable parameters. Block neural autoregressive mappings also provide differentiable log-volume information for probability transformations and accept no residual connection or a normal or gated residual connection.

- A lower Cholesky domain consists of square lower-triangular matrices with positive diagonal. Correlation matrices are symmetric positive definite matrices with unit diagonal, and their Cholesky factors additionally have unit row norms. Positive semidefinite domains allow zero eigenvalues, while positive definite domains require strictly positive eigenvalues. A unit sphere has Euclidean norm one; scaled unit lower Cholesky has the same admissible matrices as the lower Cholesky domain.

- Cholesky transformations map positive definite matrices to their lower-triangular factors, and correlation-matrix Cholesky transformations restrict that operation to correlations. Lower Cholesky affine transformation maps a vector to the supplied location plus the supplied lower-triangular factor applied to it. Simplex-to-ordered transformation maps cumulative probabilities excluding the final total to logistic quantiles shifted by a supplied anchor, whose default is zero. Recursive linear transformation returns the sequence whose current value is the supplied transition matrix applied to its preceding output plus its current input, starting from a supplied initial value that defaults to zero.

### FP-03: Composed and partially observed probability laws

- Callers can form a finite mixture from categorical mixing probabilities and either a batch of one component family or a collection of component laws with compatible events. Samples follow the weighted component law, log probabilities represent the total mixture probability, and the selected component can be returned with a draw. Incompatible component counts or event shapes are refused as incompatible mixture specifications.

- Callers can truncate normal, Cauchy, Laplace, logistic, soft Laplace, and Student t laws at a lower bound, an upper bound, or both bounds; gamma laws also support all three forms with omitted bounds for one-sided truncation and finite bounds for two-sided truncation. For ordered bounds retaining representable positive probability, draws stay within those bounds and densities are the base law conditioned on that interval. For the six symmetric families, an explicit infinite bound on one side leaves that side untruncated. Lower-truncated and doubly truncated power-law densities are proportional to the positive value raised to the supplied exponent on the supplied positive bounds. A lower-truncated law requires an exponent below negative one. A doubly truncated law accepts any finite real exponent and ordered positive bounds, including the logarithmic-uniform law at exponent negative one.

- Callers can score left-censored, right-censored, interval-censored, and doubly censored observations against a scalar base law with cumulative probabilities. An exact observation contributes its ordinary density or mass; a left-censored observation contributes probability up to its upper bound, a right-censored observation contributes probability above its lower bound, an interval observation contributes probability above its lower bound and through its upper bound, and a doubly censored observation contributes the probability outside that interval. With neither censoring indicator, endpoints whose absolute difference is no greater than one hundred-millionth plus one hundred-thousandth of the absolute upper endpoint designate an exact observation at the lower endpoint; wider ordered endpoints designate an interval observation. These probability rules apply where the involved probabilities are distinguishable from zero and one in the selected numerical precision.

- Sampling from a censored law returns uncensored draws from the base law, independently of the censoring indicators. With argument validation enabled, a base law lacking cumulative probability evaluation is refused as unsuitable for censoring.

- Callers can introduce a supplied structural-zero probability into a discrete law, including Poisson and negative binomial, and with the complementary probability follow the base law, so zero can receive both structural and base-law mass. Hurdle laws assign the supplied gate probability to zero and condition the remaining law on nonzero outcomes; built-in hurdle families are Poisson, negative binomial, gamma, and log normal. A folded scalar law produces absolute-valued draws and combines the probability from both preimages.

- A Gaussian copula combines caller-supplied scalar marginal laws and a valid correlation matrix or its Cholesky factor into a dependent joint distribution with those marginals. A beta-marginal form accepts the marginal concentrations directly.

- A boolean probability mask sets excluded log-probability contributions to zero without changing generated samples. An improper uniform law supplies zero log density on its declared support for caller-supplied values and refuses direct random sampling as unavailable; a factor-only probability law contributes its supplied log weight without a nonempty event.

- Given a base law and an ordered collection of invertible probability transformations, callers can obtain the resulting transformed law. Samples follow the transformed base law; its density is the base density at the inverse-transformed value with the corresponding probability-volume correction. Batch broadcasting and event dimensions are preserved under the transformations’ declared shape changes.

- For interval observations, a left-censoring indicator alone selects the upper endpoint probability and ignores the lower endpoint; a right-censoring indicator alone selects the probability above the lower endpoint and ignores the upper endpoint. Both indicators select the outside-interval probability and take precedence over the close-endpoint rule; neither indicator permits that rule to select an exact observation. With concrete sample validation enabled, a scored endpoint outside the base support or an interval or doubly censored observation with unordered endpoints produces a support warning and negative infinite log probability. Ignored endpoints do not invalidate a singly censored observation.

### FP-04: Probabilistic model declaration and independent data groups

- A caller can execute a probabilistic model containing named random variables, observed values, trainable parameters, deterministic values, explicit log-probability factors, and mutable auxiliary state. An observation supplies the site’s value instead of a random draw; a deterministic declaration returns its supplied value; an explicit factor contributes its supplied log weight to the model probability. A trainable parameter returns its initial or supplied value; a fitted value from one execution does not replace the initial value in an independent execution unless the caller supplies it.

- Unobserved sampling accepts an explicit random key or a surrounding source of model randomness. Ordinary model computations can use sampled and observed values to determine subsequent probability laws and return values.

- Callers can declare independent data groups, nest them on distinct batch dimensions, and stack such groups. Random variables broadcast to the declared group sizes while event dimensions remain intact. A group dimension that is not negative, or a nested group that reuses an occupied batch dimension, is refused as an invalid independence declaration.

- For a positive requested subset size no greater than the full group size, a group returns uniformly chosen distinct data indices and uses them consistently to select data and matching local parameter rows. Its probability contributions are weighted by the full group size divided by the subset size.

- Callers can mark a boolean subset of observed batch entries as known. Known entries retain their supplied observations and the remaining entries become latent values drawn from the site’s law; the combined result preserves the observation batch and event structure. During variational training, the missing entries remain latent variables rather than contributing supplied observations.

- On a local Linux CPU, fixed-structure seeded model evaluations and numerical model-probability calculations support compilation and vectorization without changing their mathematical results or batch semantics. Automatic differentiation through continuous model parameters yields derivatives of those probability calculations at differentiable interior points.

### FP-05: Controlled model execution and causal intervention

- Callers can record an execution’s named sites, their values, whether random values were observed, their probability laws, and their independent-group membership. Recording a repeated name at a random or deterministic site is refused as an ambiguous site identity.

- Callers can provide reproducible model randomness from a seed, replace selected site values by name or a caller-supplied selection rule, and reuse random values and group selections from a previous execution. Reusing a recorded value of the wrong site kind, or an observed value as an unobserved replay value, is refused as incompatible replay. Conditioning fixes selected random values as observations; removing conditioning makes observed sites random again.

- Callers can hide selected model sites from an enclosing execution observer while preserving the model’s returned computation. Selection may use a site predicate, excluded names, exposed site kinds, or exposed names, in that precedence order; with no selection, all named primitive sites are hidden. A caller can add a naming scope to separate otherwise identical submodel names.

- Callers can multiply selected probability contributions by a positive supplied weight or exclude contributions with a boolean mask. A directly supplied nonpositive weight is refused as an invalid probability scale.

- Callers can replace selected trainable parameters with prior-distributed random variables and supply per-site inference annotations for an execution. Parameter occurrences sharing a name use the same lifted value within that execution.

- A causal intervention supplies a replacement value to downstream model computations while retaining a separately observable draw or observation for the original random variable. Consequently, fixing a variable by intervention does not count the replacement as evidence for its upstream variables, whereas conditioning on that variable does.

### FP-06: Structured probabilistic sequences and conditionals

- Callers can supply a transition computation, an initial carried value, and a sequence of inputs to obtain the final carried value and the sequence of outputs. Repeated random sites have one time dimension in the execution record. Forward and reverse traversal follow the caller’s choice, with transition inputs and observations supplied inside the transition aligned to their corresponding time steps. Externally supplied latent trajectories have this time-alignment guarantee for forward traversal.

- Sequence transitions support random and deterministic sites, local independent data groups, probability masks, conditioning, replay, and supplied values under structured execution. A local group is declared inside the transition rather than around the entire sequence operation.

- Callers can select between two compatible probabilistic branches using a scalar boolean and a shared operand. With matching site identities, event structures, and distribution families across the branches, the returned value and recorded probability law are those of the selected branch. Random and deterministic branch sites support supplied values and observations.

- Requesting discrete enumeration inside a structured conditional is refused as an unsupported combination. Sequence and conditional promises cover unnested structured operations; they do not extend to enclosing independent-group contexts or branch structures that change the identities or families of random sites.

### FP-07: Model probability evaluation and valid inference coordinates

- Given model inputs and supplied latent values, callers can obtain each random site’s log-probability contribution and the joint log density. Event dimensions are reduced for each contribution, and the joint value totals all selected contributions with the declared factors, weights, masks, and observed data.

- Callers can convert latent values between their declared constrained domains and unconstrained inference coordinates and recover the original interior values. An unconstrained model potential is the negative joint log density with the probability-volume corrections for those coordinate changes. Recovered model values also include deterministic quantities evaluated from the supplied latent values.

- Callers can request initialization from supplied values, a feasible point, a prior draw, a sampled prior median, an available finite prior mean, or a uniform interval in unconstrained coordinates. Supplied values take precedence at the named sites; sites omitted from a supplied-value initialization use uniform unconstrained initialization. Uniform initialization draws each unconstrained coordinate within the caller’s symmetric radius, which defaults to two, and a zero radius selects the origin. Default inference preparation uses uniform unconstrained initialization. Sampled-median initialization uses fifteen prior draws by default and returns the coordinatewise median in unconstrained coordinates, recovered to the model’s domains. Callers may select any positive number of median-initialization draws; for an even count, each unconstrained coordinate is the average of the two middle ordered values before recovery.

- For models admitting finite joint density and finite continuous derivatives at the chosen starting values, inference preparation returns a usable starting point, potential evaluation, derivatives, and recovery of constrained model values. For initial potential derivatives in unconstrained coordinates, double-precision evaluations at finite, interior, well-conditioned starting points agree with the mathematical derivative to an absolute error no greater than 0.0002 plus 0.0002 times its magnitude, per coordinate. A low-level search reports whether its returned point is valid, including a false validity result when no valid point is found. High-level inference preparation refuses inability to initialize when that search fails.

### FP-08: Finite discrete latent inference and marginalization

- For finitely supported discrete latent variables with fixed support sizes and a tree-structured nesting of independent data groups, where dependencies within each group are elementwise and no probability outside a group depends jointly on its distinct enumerated entries, callers can request a model probability in which selected discrete variables are marginalized. The result is the total probability over their possible assignments, preserving observations, masks, weights, and the conditioning values of remaining variables.

- Given such a conditioned model, callers can recover discrete latent assignments either as posterior draws or as a joint maximum-probability assignment. The two supported inference temperatures are one for posterior sampling and zero for maximization; any other temperature is refused as unsupported.

- Finite-state Markov models with fixed state support and fixed-length observations can use structured sequence execution for marginal likelihoods. Recovery of a latent trajectory through this sequential form is supported for sequences of at most two observations. The supported sequential form has no downstream model site depending on the sequence’s final carried value.

- Callers can select tensor entries using broadcastable batches of integer indices while retaining unindexed event dimensions. Existing leading batch dimensions combine with index batch dimensions, allowing probability calculations to select components for independently batched or enumerated assignments.

- A beta prior with binomial or Bernoulli observations can be analytically collapsed when the model uses compatible symbolic probability operations. The resulting observed-data probability is the corresponding beta-binomial or integrated beta-Bernoulli probability, with the collapsed latent variable absent from the remaining sampled variables.

### FP-09: Variational objectives and trainable posterior inference

- Given a model, a guide describing a variational distribution over its latent variables, starting parameters, an optimizer, and an objective, callers can initialize training, perform updates, evaluate the current objective without updating parameters, and run a requested positive number of updates. Results contain the updated trainable parameters, objective history, and reusable training state; continued training retains the supplied fitted state. An ordinary gradient update reports the objective at its incoming parameters; training history contains the reported loss of each update. Evaluation without an update is promised for models without mutable auxiliary state; carrying such state is a training-update capability.

- The available objective families are the evidence lower bound, mean-field evidence lower bound with analytic divergence terms, dependency-aware evidence lower bound for discrete guide variables, enumerated evidence lower bound for finite discrete variables, and Rényi evidence bound. The ordinary bound estimates expected joint log probability minus guide log probability; the returned training loss has the opposite sign. Model observations, probability factors, weights, masks, and subsampled data retain their probability effects in the objective. For the Rényi objective, a subsampled independent data group must enclose every model and guide probability site; other subsampling arrangements are refused as unsupported.

- Callers can request multiple independent guide particles for a variational objective. Ordinary, mean-field, dependency-aware, and enumerated objectives use one particle by default. A guide with non-reparameterizable discrete variables can be trained with the dependency-aware objective; the enumerated objective includes the designated finite discrete uncertainty exactly rather than representing it by one sampled assignment. Ordinary and mean-field gradient training are scoped to guides with differentiable sampling for their continuous latent variables. Mutable-state training is scoped to the ordinary or mean-field objective with one particle. Finite discrete sites can designate parallel enumeration.

- Trainable model and guide parameters respect their declared domains when returned to the caller. Mutable auxiliary state is carried across supported training updates. A request to run zero or a negative number of training updates is refused as an invalid training length, and model mutable state combined with a guide that returns multiple samples per execution is refused as unsupported.

- Optimization choices comprise Adam, Adam with coordinatewise gradient clipping, Adagrad, momentum descent, RMSProp, RMSProp with momentum, stochastic gradient descent, SM3, deterministic minimization, and caller-supplied numerical optimizer transformations. For directly supplied gradient sequences, returned parameter sequences follow the mathematical optimizer definitions published in the JAX 0.12.0.1 optimizer reference, including their initial-update, stabilization, and bias-correction conventions, with the caller-supplied learning-rate schedule and numerical settings; coordinatewise clipping confines each gradient coordinate to the caller’s symmetric clipping interval before that update, with a default interval from negative ten through ten. Deterministic minimization supports deterministic objectives from point-estimate or empty guides.

- A caller can request a stable training update that retains the previous parameters and optimizer state when the proposed loss or gradient is nonfinite and returns an undefined loss to identify the rejected update; a finite valid proposal performs the ordinary update. This behavior is an explicit alternative to the ordinary training update.

- The Rényi objective accepts a finite order different from one, defaulting to zero, and uses two guide particles by default. It is the logarithm of the particle-average of the joint-to-guide probability ratios raised to one minus the order, divided by one minus the order; training returns its negative. Order one is refused as an unsupported objective order. At order zero it is the importance-weighted evidence bound. For finite, well-conditioned double-precision particle values, the returned finite Rényi loss has absolute numerical error at most 0.000002 against the mathematical particle objective.

- Training must achieve useful fits on regular, unit-scale, low-dimensional objectives with caller-selected learning rates and sufficient updates. For scalar normal-location likelihoods and interior positive or unit-interval parameters, absolute fitted-parameter error is at most 0.03; for an interior categorical-frequency objective, each fitted simplex coordinate has absolute error at most 0.01. Deterministic minimization and compatible caller-supplied optimizer transformations fit a scalar normal location within 0.01. For a binary latent variable with a nondegenerate posterior and a trainable binary guide, dependency-aware training with hundreds of independent particles per update recovers posterior probability within 0.055. Errors are measured against the objective minimizer or exact posterior probability, independently of the training trajectory.

### FP-10: Automatic posterior guide construction

- For a fixed-structure model with continuous latent variables, callers can generate a variational guide without writing a separate latent-variable model. Posterior draws preserve original latent identities, event shapes, and constrained supports, using the caller’s supplied guide parameters.

- The available approximation families are per-site independent normal, joint diagonal normal, full-covariance multivariate normal, low-rank multivariate normal, batched full-covariance normal, batched low-rank normal, point estimate, Laplace approximation, inverse autoregressive flow normal, block neural autoregressive flow normal, differentiable annealed importance sampling, surrogate-likelihood differentiable annealed importance sampling, and a semi-parametric annealed approximation with separate local and global guides. Batched normal guides retain the declared common batch shape; full-covariance and low-rank guides can represent correlated latent coordinates.

- For unconstrained latent coordinates and elementwise monotone constrained coordinates, normal guide families provide posterior marginal medians. The unbatched per-site, diagonal, full-covariance, and low-rank normal families additionally provide requested marginal quantiles for those coordinates. Point-estimate guides return their fitted point. For a smooth model with positive definite negative log-posterior curvature at a fitted mode, the Laplace guide supplies the local normal posterior approximation about that mode. For a regular low-dimensional Gaussian posterior, a fitted Laplace location is within 0.003 of the mode and its covariance equals the inverse negative log-posterior curvature at that location.

- Callers can combine separately supplied guides over disjoint selected model parts into one guide, with posterior draws containing the union of their latent values. Ordinary, surrogate-likelihood, and semi-parametric annealed automatic guides are refused as unsupported parts of a combined guide. An automatic guide part that exposes deterministic model sites is refused as an incompatible guide part.

- Continuous automatic guides refuse unresolved discrete latent sites as unsupported supports, and joint continuous guides refuse models with no latent variables as an empty latent space. Normal guide initialization with a nonpositive scale is refused as an invalid approximation scale. Surrogate-likelihood and semi-parametric annealed guides support data-subsampled training with independent data groups, local latent variables for the sampled data where present, and a caller-supplied global guide in the semi-parametric form; ordinary joint annealed guides are scoped to full-data models.

- Per-site independent and joint diagonal normal guides use supplied unconstrained locations and positive standard deviations; full-covariance normal guides use a supplied covariance factor. For low-rank guides, the supplied factor rows are in units of each coordinate’s supplied positive standard deviation: the covariance contribution is the cross-product of the factor after scaling each row by that standard deviation, added to the diagonal of squared standard deviations. These laws and their marginal summaries are recovered to each latent variable’s constrained domain with the publicly available reversible mapping for that support, including its probability-volume correction. An omitted initialization scale gives covariance 0.01 times the identity in unconstrained coordinates for each normal family.

- On well-conditioned unit-scale low-dimensional Gaussian posteriors in real or log coordinates, automatic guides trained with a suitable caller-selected optimizer must provide useful finite approximations. With at least 512 posterior draws, each empirical coordinate mean is within 0.22 of its analytic target and each non-point coordinate variance within 0.09; a point estimate targets the mode in the original domain. The fitted marginal cumulative probability differs from the target by at most 0.10, with empirical comparisons additionally allowing independent-sampling uncertainty at failure probability one in a trillion. For scalar conjugate Gaussian inference with full-data or surrogate annealed guides and at least 8192 draws, empirical posterior mean and variance errors are at most 0.16 and 0.06, and corresponding unit-noise posterior predictive errors are at most 0.18 and 0.15. In the semi-parametric form, local mean and variance errors are at most 0.20 and 0.16, global mean and variance errors at most 0.12 and 0.10, and independent-component correlation magnitude at most 0.15. These are approximation effects after sufficient training, not a prescribed optimization trajectory.

### FP-11: Probability-preserving model reparameterization

- Callers can choose a reparameterization for selected latent sites and obtain the original model variables as recovered values with the same joint probability measure. Available choices are location-scale decentering, transformed-distribution coordinates, projected-normal coordinates, circular coordinates, an explicit caller-supplied invertible transform, unit-volume transforms, Haar coordinates, type-II discrete cosine coordinates, and neural transport from a fitted continuous automatic guide. For an explicit caller-supplied transformation, the new coordinate is that transformation applied to the original value and recovery returns its inverse. Unit-volume event transformations act on unconstrained event coordinates.

- Location-scale decentering accepts a centering fraction from zero through one, including a trainable fraction, and preserves the original location-scale distribution. A supplied fraction outside that interval is refused as invalid centering. This mode is scoped to unobserved location-scale families; a transformed distribution must use its corresponding transformed-distribution mode. Zero centering uses the standardized location-scale coordinate, while full centering uses the original coordinate. Intermediate fractions give nontrivial partial centering, and changing a trainable fraction can change that mapping.

- Transformed-distribution reparameterization samples the base latent law and recovers the original transformed value. Circular and projected-normal reparameterizations preserve their circular and spherical output laws. Applying these latent-only modes to an observed site is refused as an unsupported observation. For a regular von Mises target, sine and cosine means pooled over eight independently keyed chains of 1200 retained draws after 300 warmup draws have absolute error at most 0.09 against the target moments.

- Neural transport accepts a fitted joint continuous automatic guide with a reversible transform independent of model call arguments and returns recovered original latent samples after inference in the transported model. Guides without such a transform are refused as incompatible transport guides. The supported model has fixed latent structure. On a well-conditioned low-dimensional Gaussian posterior, pooling eight independently keyed chains of 2000 retained draws after 300 warmup draws gives posterior mean and posterior-centered second-moment errors at most 0.15 per element.

- Haar, discrete cosine, and other unit-volume latent reparameterizations preserve the probability measure over the specified event dimensions and recover the original event values. They refuse observed sites as unsupported observations.

### FP-12: Posterior Markov chain sampling and conditional blocks

- Given a model with observations or a caller-supplied continuous potential, random keys, a warmup length, and a sampling length, callers can collect correlated draws targeting that posterior or potential-defined probability law. Collected model values obey their original constraints and include derived deterministic values. Warmup draws are excluded from posterior draws unless explicitly requested as a separate warmup collection.

- Continuous posterior sampling supports differentiable targets and targets for which continuous derivatives are unavailable. Ensemble forms evolve a caller-supplied collection of initial chains. A potential-only request must supply initial latent coordinates, and omitting them is refused as missing initialization.

- Callers can run multiple chains and retrieve draws grouped by chain or with chain and draw dimensions combined. CPU execution supports both separate chains and jointly batched chains. They can request retained draws at a positive integer thinning interval and obtain selected per-draw sampling diagnostics such as potential energy, acceptance information, and divergences. For each chain, the retained draw count is the whole-number part of the requested post-warmup sampling length divided by the thinning interval.

- Callers can perform warmup separately and continue from the previous sampling state for the same target and model inputs. Continued runs preserve the supplied latent state. Changed model inputs are supported in a fresh run without a reused warmup or final state; posterior probabilities and deterministic values then reflect the new inputs.

- Finitely supported discrete variables selected for marginalization are integrated out of the continuous sampling target. Mixed discrete-continuous Hamiltonian sampling and discrete Gibbs updates additionally return discrete latent draws alongside continuous ones. Callers can also supply conditional updates for selected latent variables.

- Callers can compose conditional sampling blocks over disjoint subsets of one model’s latent variables, including nested compositions, automatic discrete blocks, user-supplied conditional updates, and continuous Hamiltonian blocks. Each update conditions on the current values outside its owned subset. At most one block can claim all remaining variables. Enumerated variables are excluded from block ownership.

- A conditional composition is refused as an invalid partition when explicit ownership overlaps, leaves a non-enumerated latent variable unowned, names a non-latent site, or gives an explicitly selected block no variables. Blocks over different models, continuous Hamiltonian ownership of discrete variables, general block composition with data subsampling, and continuous block supports whose bounds depend on other latent values are refused as incompatible sampling specifications. A user update returning missing or extra owned variables is refused as an invalid conditional update.

- Energy-conserving subsampling Hamiltonian inference supports continuous models with declared subsampled independent data groups and returns posterior draws together with the selected data subsets. Ordinary general conditional block composition does not gain subsampling support from this specialized mode.

- Ensemble sampling requires jointly batched execution of an even number of chains greater than one, with initial values carrying the same chain count; incompatible chain mode, count, or initial batch dimensions are refused. By default, one chain is run and every post-warmup draw is retained. A warmup length that is not a nonnegative integer, a thinning interval that is not a positive integer, or a sampling length that is not an integer at least as large as the thinning interval is refused as an invalid collection specification. The even-chain condition is necessary but proposal-specific prerequisites also apply: the stretch and random-direction proposals support two chains, while a differential proposal requiring two distinct complementary chains requires at least four total chains.

- Callers can use externally supplied probability laws and transition kernels through the supported numerical probability integration. Those laws can serve as model variables and those kernels can drive the posterior sampling workflow. Posterior values retain the supplied laws and their declared supports.

- Finite posterior collections must achieve coarse distribution accuracy on well-conditioned low-dimensional Gaussian, beta-binomial and Laplace targets. With at least 1000 retained draws from regular targets after warmup, empirical mean and standard-deviation errors are each below 20% of the target standard deviation. With at least 12000 retained draws pooled across independently keyed runs after at least 300 warmup transitions, empirical cumulative probabilities at the tenth, twenty-fifth, fiftieth, seventy-fifth and ninetieth percentiles differ by less than 0.04. Individual-chain and continuation collections of at least 1000 draws allow cumulative-probability error below 0.10; a short scalar Gaussian collection of at least 800 retained draws has absolute mean error below 0.15. For unit-scale paired Gaussian conditional models, each covariance element has absolute error below 0.16. For a regular binary–Gaussian mixture posterior, pooled continuous mean and variance errors are below 0.18 and 0.20, central cumulative-probability errors below 0.10, and binary posterior probability error below 0.10; conditional Gaussian draws meet the same relative moment and cumulative-probability accuracy. These are empirical accuracy promises for correlated samples, not independence guarantees.

### FP-13: Prior and posterior prediction and observation likelihoods

- Callers can generate prior predictive draws from a generative model, or posterior predictive draws conditioned on caller-supplied latent samples or draws from a supplied guide with fitted parameters. The predictive distribution uses the model’s current inputs and laws for unobserved outputs.

- By default, prediction returns newly generated random values and deterministic model quantities while omitting latent values already supplied for conditioning. Explicit site selection can include those latent values. Deterministic quantities are recomputed from the current inputs by default; a caller can explicitly choose to condition on supplied deterministic values instead.

- Prediction retains the caller’s designated leading posterior-sample dimensions and appends each site’s batch and event dimensions. A request without supplied posterior samples must specify the predictive draw count, including when a guide is supplied; absence of both posterior samples and a specified draw count is refused as an incomplete prediction request. For a positive specified count, the result contains that many predictive draws. If a separately requested draw count disagrees with the count of consistently shaped supplied posterior samples, prediction warns and uses the posterior sample count. Supplied sites with different leading posterior-sample shapes are refused as incompatible conditioning batches.

- Prediction can recover finitely supported discrete latent variables from their posterior conditional on supplied continuous values. Returned discrete draws and subsequent generated observations follow that conditional model rather than replacing discrete uncertainty with fixed prior assignments.

- Given observations and supplied posterior values, callers can obtain observation log likelihoods separately for each observed site, posterior draw, and independent observation batch entry. These values score the observed law without adding prior-site contributions.

### FP-14: Posterior summaries and inference quality diagnostics

- For sample arrays with caller-designated chain and draw dimensions, callers can obtain per-component mean, sample standard deviation, median, the shortest interval between sample values whose rank separation is the whole-number part of the requested probability mass times the sample count, effective sample size, and ordinary or split Gelman–Rubin convergence measures. Summaries retain the original parameter component structure and can be printed or returned as data. Summary intervals default to probability mass nine tenths; explicitly requested interval masses are greater than zero and less than one. A sample that is not a number makes the corresponding component’s median undefined rather than being silently removed.

- Callers can obtain autocovariance and autocorrelation by lag along a selected sample dimension, choosing whether normalization uses the full sample count or the number of overlapping pairs. The full sample count is used by default. Autocovariance measures mean-centered products of overlapping observations, and autocorrelation divides each lag’s covariance by the lag-zero covariance. Effective sample size accounts for serial correlation according to the effective-sample-size definition in the Stan Reference Manual, version 2.18, applied to the caller’s chain-and-draw arrays and selected covariance normalization. Ordinary Gelman–Rubin is the square root of the ratio of the pooled variance estimate to mean within-chain sample variance; the pooled estimate weights that within-chain variance by one minus the reciprocal draw count and adds the sample variance of chain means. The split form applies that measure to the first and last equally sized halves of each chain, omitting the middle draw for an odd draw count. Ordinary Gelman–Rubin requires at least two chains and two draws per chain; the split form requires at least four draws. Effective sample size requires at least two draws per chain. Requests below these prerequisites are refused as insufficient chains or draws.

- Callers can combine independent subposterior draws with a Gaussian consensus approximation, obtain a combined Gaussian location and covariance, or draw from that Gaussian approximation. Full-covariance and diagonal-covariance forms are available; independent normal subposteriors combine according to their relative precision. Finite supplied populations use their empirical means and sample covariances with sample-count-minus-one normalization. The combined precision is the sum of their precisions, and the combined location is their precision-weighted mean. Without requested resampling, each consensus draw combines the supplied draws at that same index using those precision weights.

- For a model, guide, fitted parameters, and observations, callers can estimate the Pareto importance-weight tail diagnostic described by Vehtari and colleagues, Pareto Smoothed Importance Sampling, Journal of Machine Learning Research 25(72), 2024, using posterior-guide draws and the model-joint-to-guide probability ratios. The returned estimate characterizes the upper tail of those ratios. Fewer than two requested particles are refused as insufficient samples; exactly equal evaluated importance ratios for all sampled particles produce a warning and an infinite diagnostic rather than a finite reliability claim. For exact Pareto importance-ratio tails with shape from 0.2 through 0.7, estimates from 200000 independent guide particles have absolute shape error below 0.25.

- For a generative model with continuous latent variables, a guide, an optimizer, and a variational objective, callers can obtain variational simulation-based calibration probabilities and ranks for each latent component. Results include a two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov symmetry assessment of those probabilities against their reflections about one half, using the asymptotic assessment for the discrete empirical probabilities.

- Calibration supports explicitly selected data sites and caller-provided conversion of simulated data into model and guide inputs, including container-valued observations. It refuses unknown data sites, a model with no remaining latent variables, discrete latent sites, ambiguous automatic data routing, or a conversion that does not return positional and named arguments. Calibration requires at least two simulations, at least one training update per simulation, and at least two fitted-guide draws; requests below these limits are refused as insufficient calibration work.

- For variational calibration, each simulation draws generating latent values and data from the model, fits the supplied guide to that data for the requested number of updates, and compares the fitted-guide draws to that simulation’s generating latent values. A component’s rank is the count of guide draws strictly below its generating value, and its calibration probability is that count divided by the number of guide draws. With 2048 independent guide draws in regular normal calibration models, empirical component probabilities differ from the fitted-guide cumulative probabilities at the generating values by at most 0.10. Observation noise negligible relative to guide scale may contribute only its corresponding negligible probability error.

- Without an explicit calibration data conversion, direct observation arguments whose names match selected data sites are filled from the corresponding simulated values. A single remaining unmatched data site can fill a single unset direct argument; other unmatched or already-bound data arguments require the caller’s conversion. With no explicit data-site selection, the model must expose at least one observed random site; otherwise calibration is refused as missing data-site selection.

### FP-15: Model dependency inspection and visualization

- For a fixed-structure model and its call inputs, callers can obtain prior dependencies of latent and observed variables and posterior dependencies between latent variables after conditioning on observations. Dependencies identify the independent data groups that become coupled by a relationship; conditioning on a shared dependent observation can introduce dependence between its previously independent parents.

- Callers can obtain relationships between random sites, trainable parameters, and independent data groups, together with distribution families, parameter constraints, and observed-versus-latent distinctions. A probability mask on observations does not prevent dependency inspection.

- Callers can produce a directed model graph that represents random-site dependencies and independent groups and distinguishes observed variables. They can choose whether trainable parameters, distribution annotations, and parameter constraints appear, and save the graph as a rendered image using an available local renderer. Graph promises cover models without structured sequence execution.

- Callers can obtain a shape report for an execution record, distinguishing distribution batch dimensions, event dimensions, recorded value dimensions, and optionally log-probability dimensions for each recorded random site. The report preserves which dimensions belong to each site rather than returning one combined model shape.

### FP-16: Neural networks in probabilistic models

- Callers can register a neural network as a named component of a probabilistic model or guide through functional initialization-and-application or object-based numerical network interfaces. Its trainable numerical parameters are exposed to probabilistic training, and evaluating the registered network with the same parameters and inputs preserves its ordinary neural network output. On well-conditioned realizable affine regression with caller-selected training, maximum absolute network-output error is at most 0.02 on training inputs and 0.03 on held-out inputs in the same bounded input domain.

- Object-based network integrations support caller-supplied priors on selected network parameters, including a prior determined from the parameter name and shape. Those parameters become latent random values with their original shapes; unselected parameters retain their ordinary values. Repeated evaluations of one registered network retain its bound parameter values within the model execution.

- Object-based networks can carry mutable network state through single-particle variational training updates, and stochastic network layers accept caller-selected randomness. Bayesian network draws can be used for model inference and posterior prediction while preserving network input and output shapes. Dropout independently zeros each activation with the caller-selected probability and scales retained activations by the reciprocal survival probability; independent caller keys give independent masks. For a well-conditioned conjugate Gaussian affine-network posterior, 1600 retained draws after 300 warmup transitions give absolute parameter-mean error at most 0.04 and covariance error per element at most 0.004 plus 0.25 times the magnitude of its analytic covariance.

- Registering a new functional network requires existing supplied parameter values or the input shape needed to initialize them. A request lacking both is refused as missing network initialization information.

### FP-17: Stein particle posterior approximation

- Given a model, an optimizer, a particle count, and a Stein kernel, callers can fit a particle-based posterior approximation, inspect the fitted particles or mixture-guide parameters, and continue training through successive updates of the returned state. Both point particles and mixtures of guide distributions are supported; an annealed point-particle form accepts caller-selected cycles and transition speed and also supports continuation of a batch run from its returned state. Evaluation, updates, and training history expose the nonnegative Euclidean norm of the Stein particle force as a diagnostic. Each update and its history entry report the force at the incoming particle state. Repeating initialization with the same key and model inputs yields the same initial particles. For a single ordinary point particle with a Gaussian radial basis kernel and a normal target, this is the absolute normal log-density derivative at that particle.

- Stein mixture inference accepts a compatible guide and produces a mixture of guide distributions parameterized by the fitted particles. Predictive draws select all particle components with equal probability and follow the selected component's guide distribution, then the model's conditional observation distribution using fitted shared parameters, retaining posterior-site and observation shapes.

- The available kernel families are Gaussian radial basis, inverse multiquadric, linear, random-feature, mixture, graphical, unnormalized Gaussian probability-product, and radial Gauss–Newton. Gaussian radial basis supports scalar, coordinatewise vector, and diagonal-matrix outputs; inverse multiquadric supports scalar and coordinatewise vector outputs; graphical kernels provide coordinate blocks; linear, random-feature, probability-product, and radial Gauss–Newton forms are scalar. A mixture requires at least two component kernels with the same output form and returns their caller-weighted sum without normalizing the weights. Inverse multiquadric requires a positive scale constant and an exponent strictly between negative one and zero, defaulting to one and negative one half respectively; invalid values are refused as invalid kernel specifications. The linear kernel is the inner product plus one.

  Omitted output selection gives a scalar. Gaussian radial basis matrix output can repeat the scalar similarity or place coordinatewise similarities on the diagonal; omitting this choice repeats the scalar similarity.

- Stein mixture inference refuses flow guides and the ordinary, surrogate-likelihood, and semi-parametric annealed automatic guides as incompatible. Fixed-value, feasible-point, and zero-radius initializations that give coincident particle locations are refused as unsuitable particle initialization. Annealed Stein inference refuses a nonpositive cycle count or transition speed.

- Gaussian radial basis kernel values are the exponential of negative squared distance divided by a bandwidth, applied either to the full vector or separately to each coordinate. For more than one particle the bandwidth is the squared median of all ordered pairwise Euclidean distances, including self-distances, multiplied by a caller-supplied function of particle count; that function defaults to the reciprocal of the natural logarithm of the count. For one particle the bandwidth is one. Inverse multiquadric raises the sum of the squared scale constant and squared distance to the supplied exponent, with distance reduced over the full vector or left coordinatewise according to the selected output form.

- Graphical kernels return a block-diagonal similarity over caller-designated parameter groups, using the selected local kernel for each group and the default local kernel for groups without an override. The default local kernel is Gaussian radial basis. The unnormalized Gaussian probability-product kernel is restricted to per-site independent normal guides; its value is the exponential of negative one half times the supplied exponent scale times the sum, over coordinates, of squared differences in guide locations divided by the sum of the corresponding guide variances. The exponent scale defaults to one.

- A random-feature kernel provides a random positive semidefinite similarity on the supplied particle coordinates. With all particles used, positive finite bandwidth, and well-conditioned unit-scale coordinates, similarity is nonconstant in coordinates; at two separated coordinates, two independent random initializations give different representable similarity values with probability at least one half. This nondegeneracy condition permits either discrete or continuous random similarity laws. For a finite double-precision similarity matrix at well-conditioned coordinates, its smallest eigenvalue is greater than negative 0.00000001, allowing numerical roundoff in this property. Initialization with the same random key and particle shape is reproducible; after initialization, repeated evaluation on the same two coordinates gives the same similarity. Evaluating without initialization is refused as missing random-kernel initialization, and a particle array with a different shape is refused as incompatible particles.

- For a caller-supplied differentiable scalar particle loss, the radial Gauss–Newton kernel gives Gaussian similarity according to loss-gradient directions: its exponent is negative one divided by twice the particle dimension times the average squared directional derivative along the separation of the two queried particles, with one loss gradient evaluated at each supplied particle.

- On well-conditioned unit-scale scalar Gaussian targets, a suitable caller-selected optimizer with sufficient training must yield useful fitted locations. With at least 64 ordinary point particles, empirical cumulative probabilities at central target probabilities from 0.001 through 0.999 differ by less than 0.18. For a mixture of normal guides, the average fitted component location differs from the target mean by less than 0.3; for a single annealed point, location differs from the target mode by less than 0.1. After changing the target and continuing training through state updates, the average fitted location differs from the new target mean by less than 0.3. These are approximation effects rather than a specified optimization trajectory; full distributional calibration is required here only for the ordinary point-particle form.

### FP-18: Inference for models with stochastic support

- For a model whose explicitly designated discrete branching choices select paths with different latent sites or dimensions, callers can obtain path-specific posterior results and normalized nonnegative path weights. Supported paths have a fixed ordered set of branching decisions; the continuous latent structure within each conditioned path is fixed.

- A sampling-based form returns path-specific posterior samples and relative posterior mass estimates. Quantitative sampling-mass accuracy is supported for well-conditioned Gaussian paths with common continuous dimension and scale: each normalized mass estimate has absolute error less than 0.08 against the posterior mass among discovered paths. Paths of differing dimensions retain local posterior inference and normalized weights without this mass-accuracy guarantee. A variational form returns fitted path-specific guides and parameters, with weights proportional to the exponentiated path evidence-bound estimates; on well-conditioned Gaussian paths with fitted diagonal normal guides, each normalized weight differs by less than 0.04 from independently evaluated normalized exponentiated bounds of those guides.

- For a positive caller-selected exploration budget and path cap, inference returns at least one discovered path and no more paths than either limit. Each returned path’s result retains that path’s own latent names and shapes.

- A continuously distributed site designated as a branching choice is refused as unsupported branching. Support-decomposition variational inference accepts ordinary, mean-field, dependency-aware, and enumerated evidence-bound objectives, and refuses other objective families as incompatible.

- Local inference must give useful posterior approximations on well-conditioned, low-dimensional conjugate Gaussian paths, including discovered paths with different dimensions. For at least 6000 post-warmup sampling transitions after at least 400 warmup transitions, each marginal empirical mean and standard deviation differ from the exact posterior values by less than 0.20 posterior standard deviations, and empirical cumulative probabilities at target probabilities 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 0.9 differ by less than 0.07. With a suitable caller-selected optimizer and sufficient training, a normal guide's marginal cumulative probability differs from the target by less than 0.10; empirical comparisons from independent guide draws additionally allow sampling uncertainty at failure probability one in a trillion per comparison. These sampling and fitting guarantees describe high-probability approximation quality, not fixed random trajectories or a guarantee for arbitrary multimodal models.

### FP-19: Hilbert space Gaussian process models

- Callers can obtain Gaussian-process function draws at supplied input locations using a finite Hilbert space approximation. Supported covariance families are squared exponential, Matérn, one-dimensional rational quadratic, and one-dimensional periodic squared exponential. The function law follows the supplied amplitude, length scale, approximation domain, and approximation size; Matérn also accepts smoothness and rational quadratic accepts its scale-mixture shape. For the nonperiodic families, amplitude denotes the unapproximated covariance at zero separation; for periodic spectral weights it denotes the square root of that covariance. Periodic covariance accepts its fundamental angular frequency in radians per input unit. For the periodic finite function law, the cited periodic weights are Gaussian coefficient standard deviations, rather than coefficient variances; thus each weight contributes its square to function covariance.

- For rectangular one-dimensional or multidimensional domains with Dirichlet boundaries, callers can obtain eigenfunction values, associated frequencies, and covariance spectral weights. Their mathematical definitions are those of Solin and Särkkä, Hilbert Space Methods for Reduced-Rank Gaussian Process Regression, Statistics and Computing 30, 2020, and Riutort-Mayol and colleagues, Practical Hilbert Space Approximate Bayesian Gaussian Processes for Probabilistic Programming, Statistics and Computing 33, article 17, 2023. Inputs are the caller’s locations, domain half-width for each axis, retained term count for each axis, covariance family, amplitude, length scales, and family-specific smoothness or scale-mixture shape. Periodic values use the latter publication’s Appendix B with the supplied one-dimensional locations, frequency, amplitude, length scale, and retained term count. Rational-quadratic spectral and function-law guarantees are limited to one-dimensional locations.

- Squared exponential and Matérn models accept scalar or per-axis length scales and scalar or per-axis domain widths and term counts. Squared exponential and Matérn function draws have zero mean. The one-dimensional rational-quadratic law uses a positive length scale and a scale-mixture shape greater than one half; its scale-mixture shape is not a differentiable inference input.

- An input dimension inconsistent with the supplied per-axis domain widths, term counts, or length scales is refused as an incompatible Gaussian-process specification. Periodic eigenfunctions refuse multidimensional input locations as unsupported; the periodic approximation is scoped to one-dimensional inputs.

### FP-20: Nested posterior sampling and evidence estimation

- For models with proper priors, callers can obtain weighted posterior samples, obtain a requested number of resampled unweighted posterior draws, and inspect estimated model evidence and sampling diagnostics through nested inference. Supported scalar priors are normal, Cauchy, half normal, half Cauchy, Student t, Laplace, asymmetric Laplace in either parameterization, soft Laplace, logistic, uniform, log uniform, log normal, exponential, gamma, inverse gamma, chi-squared, beta in either parameterization, Pareto, Gumbel, Gompertz, Lévy, Dagum, and discrete uniform. Multivariate normal, Dirichlet, Bernoulli, and categorical priors are also supported, as are invertible transformations and independent or expanded versions of those laws.

- Selected finitely supported discrete latent variables can be marginalized in the nested-inference likelihood. Posterior output recovers the model’s original constrained variables and deterministic quantities, and resampled draws preserve each site’s event shape.

- The caller can obtain an inference summary and diagnostic plots after results exist. Completed-run sample counts, likelihood-evaluation counts and effective sample size are positive. On well-conditioned low-dimensional models, posterior marginal histogram cumulative probabilities differ from the posterior by less than 0.15 at central probabilities from 0.25 through 0.75. Asking for posterior samples, a result summary, or result plots without a completed run is refused as missing inference results.

- On well-conditioned low-dimensional posterior problems, nested inference with at least 1200 live points and a requested log-evidence stopping uncertainty no greater than 0.001 must achieve useful probability accuracy. At finite marginal thresholds, absolute marginal cumulative-probability error is less than 0.10 for weighted scalar output and less than 0.13 for at least 16000 resampled draws and for multivariate, transformed or marginalized output. On smooth scalar Gaussian conditioning problems, absolute estimated log-evidence error is less than 0.30. These are high-probability approximation guarantees measured against the exact posterior and marginal likelihood, without fixing a random trajectory or treating weighted nested points as independent draws.
